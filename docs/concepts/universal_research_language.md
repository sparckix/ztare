# Primitive Glossary - Universal Research Language

This glossary is the public, non-technical version of the universal research
language. It names recurring moves that show up when people make progress on
hard research problems.

The point is practical: a named move is easier to notice, teach, audit, and
stress-test. The names are not proof that a move is correct. They are handles
for asking: what does this move owe next, and what would show that it failed?

## Current Status

The latest reliability work changed how this glossary should be read. The fine
primitive names below are useful, but they are not a clean one-label taxonomy.
Two competent readers often agree that a move is covered by the language while
choosing nearby fine labels.

The more reliable layer is the four obligation classes:

1. **Construct** - build the missing object or witness.
2. **Transfer** - carry a method, claim, or structure across a boundary.
3. **Bound** - prove that the needed quantity is controlled.
4. **Decompose** - split a mixed object into owned parts and recombine them.

Use these four classes as the first routing layer. Use the fine primitive names
as a checklist, a retrieval aid, and a way to catch near-confusions. A single
research move can carry more than one obligation.

Evidence status, as of 2026-05-23: fine primitive identity reached moderate
inter-rater agreement on the 118-move corpus (Cohen kappa 0.578). The four
obligation classes improved reliability to kappa 0.741. That is usable for
routing with caution, not a license to force one exclusive label.

Update as of 2026-05-24: the clearest agent-facing evidence is not label
recovery. It is contract preservation. Pattern/action contracts improved
downstream handoff quality in synthetic, second-stage, and real NS-trace
consumer tests by preserving the required carrier slots, confuser checks, and
artifact shape. Anti-patterns helped most when they selected the exact
preventive receipt that was missing or already paid. The orchestration menu did
not show live improvement over memory/context on short packets, so it should be
read as a checklist and sequencing surface, not as a proven performance source.

## How To Use This Glossary

When reading a research move, ask four questions first:

- Does it need to build something concrete? Use **Construct**.
- Does it move something from one setting to another? Use **Transfer**.
- Does it need a quantitative control, budget, or threshold? Use **Bound**.
- Does it need to split a combined object into parts? Use **Decompose**.

Then use the fine primitives below to ask sharper questions. The safest use is
not "which label wins?" but "what artifact or check does this label force?"
For agent or RD workflows, write that answer as a contract: required carrier,
source-bound action target, nearest confuser, downstream consumer check, and
clean-proceed condition when a risk has already been paid.

## The Four Obligation Classes

### Construct

A move has a construct obligation when progress depends on producing an object,
witness, example, counterexample, dataset row, proof packet, or executable
artifact.

Typical owed artifact: a named object with declared properties, admissibility
checks, and a verification step.

Common failure: talking about the object class without producing one specimen
that actually satisfies the needed conditions.

### Transfer

A move has a transfer obligation when it imports a method, analogy, proxy,
theorem, measurement, or frame from one setting into another.

Typical owed artifact: a source-to-target map, the assumptions that survive,
the assumptions that break, and the target-side test that would reject the
transfer.

Common failure: treating resemblance as evidence before checking what the
target setting actually preserves.

### Bound

A move has a bound obligation when it needs a quantity to stay below, above, or
within a budget: an inequality, threshold, rate, margin, exchange rate, error
term, or accumulated cost.

Typical owed artifact: normalized variables, the target inequality, step-by-step
bookkeeping, and the first line where the estimate succeeds or fails.

Common failure: paying in one currency and spending in another without proving
the exchange rate.

### Decompose

A move has a decompose obligation when a mixed phenomenon must be separated
into components, regimes, cases, owners, carriers, or branches before a claim
can be trusted.

Typical owed artifact: the component list, ownership rule, per-part check,
recombination rule, and cancellation or double-counting audit.

Common failure: drawing a conclusion from the final aggregate while ignoring
which part produced it.

## Fine Primitive Names

These are the finer handles mined from mathematical and research corpora. They
are descriptive handles, not exclusive bins.

### Shared Core Operations

These appeared across most of the mathematical subfields studied.

1. **Problem Reformulation and Reduction** - replace the original problem with
   a connected version that is easier to attack or verify.
2. **Generalization and Abstraction** - enlarge the class of objects so several
   cases become one framework.
3. **Decomposition and Recomposition** - break the problem into parts, solve or
   inspect the parts, then put the answer back together.
4. **Local-to-Global Assembly** - prove or build small pieces first, then show
   they cohere into a global result.
5. **Canonical Form and Invariance** - remove irrelevant representation choices
   and keep only the stable content.
6. **Cross-Domain Translation** - build a formal bridge that lets a method or
   theorem from one domain act in another.

### Broadly Shared Operations

These appeared in several subfields but not as universally as the shared core.

1. **Iterative Refinement** - repeatedly improve an approximation under a
   monitored rule.
2. **Recursive Decomposition** - reduce the problem to smaller versions of
   itself until a base case is reached.
3. **Duality and Adversarial Framing** - study a paired, opposite, or game-like
   version of the problem to expose the original structure.
4. **Layered Approximation and Convergence** - build a sequence of simpler
   approximations and control the limit.
5. **Extremal Method** - study boundary, best-case, or worst-case examples to
   force a general conclusion.
6. **Probabilistic and Stochastic Methods** - use randomness, sampling, or
   expectation to prove existence or control behavior.
7. **Dimensional and Structural Lifting** - move the problem into a larger or
   richer space where the constraint becomes easier to see.
8. **Constraint Imposition and Propagation** - impose required conditions and
   follow their consequences until the structure is forced or contradicted.

### Peripheral or Field-Specific Idioms

These are useful handles but should be treated with extra transfer caution.

1. **Characterization by Obstruction** - define a class by what it forbids.
2. **Internalization and Self-Reference** - encode a system's own behavior
   inside the system.
3. **Axiomatization and Foundational Repair** - repair or state the ground rules
   of the formal system.
4. **Controlled Universe Extension** - add objects to a universe while preserving
   selected rules and forcing a chosen outcome.

## Meta-Language Moves

Some moves change the research game itself, not only the object inside the
game. These are rarer and need stricter evidence.

1. **Admissible-Criteria Rebaselining** - change what counts as a legitimate
   object, solution, or proof.
2. **Structural-Semantics Pluralization** - treat meaning as invariance across
   transformations, models, gauges, or representations.
3. **Ontological Promotion** - turn a bookkeeping device, auxiliary object, or
   limit artifact into a primary object of study.

Current status: these are narrow research handles. They are most credible when
compiled into explicit receipt fields, nearest-confuser checks, and a downstream
consumer handoff.

## PDE Estimate-Craft Handles

PDE work exposed a boundary of the general catalogue. Some estimate-building
moves need field-native execution contracts, not just universal labels. The
portable handles currently used with caution are:

1. **Auxiliary Comparison Object** - build a barrier, weight, profile, or
   comparison object that makes the estimate visible.
2. **Regime or Boundary Split** - split the problem into cases where different
   estimates apply.
3. **Threshold Dichotomy** - prove that either a threshold is crossed or a
   stronger fallback holds.
4. **Limit-Passage Inheritance** - show that a property survives approximation,
   rescaling, compactness, or weak convergence.
5. **Sharpness or Counterpacket Witness** - build an example showing where a
   claimed estimate breaks or becomes tight.

For PDE use, the name is never enough. The owed artifact is an estimate
derivation, hostile packet, theorem-applicability match, or strictly smaller
remaining theorem.

## What This Glossary Does Not Claim

- It does not claim these names make agents solve hard problems by themselves.
- It does not claim the fine primitive catalogue is mutually exclusive.
- It does not claim every field uses the same moves at the same grain.
- It does not turn analogies into evidence.

The strongest current claim is narrower: the language helps when it becomes an
action contract that survives handoff. The useful question is: what concrete
artifact, check, bound, witness, preventive receipt, or falsifier does this move
force next, and can another worker recover that obligation without rereading the
whole research arc?
