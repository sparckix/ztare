# Graded reasoning

> Up: [Documentation map](../README.md)

A governed research map — theses, claims, evidence, tensions, and the edges between them — is a bipolar argument graph: some edges support, some attack. The argument kernel (`src/ztare/scenarios/argument_kernel.py`) resolves it to a crisp verdict, `SUPPORTED` / `BLOCKED` / `REFUTED`, by grounded (least-fixpoint) acceptance over an assumption-based argumentation framework. That verdict is the right floor — monotone in warrant strength — but the wrong headline on a research substrate, where almost nothing is ever fully settled. Nearly every live map reads `BLOCKED`, and a crisp `BLOCKED` cannot distinguish a thesis backed by nine sources with eleven open challenges (the verified `ai_capex` case) from three theses with no support at all. Both read identically.

The kernel's edges already carry what is needed to grade that distinction: a Toulmin warrant typed by re-executable checkability, not belief. `W0` is a kernel certificate — a LeanMill proof or an `axiom_authority` record. `W1` is a re-executable computation that recomputes from bound data. `W2` is a verbatim quote binding. `W3` is an LLM-proposed edge, admitted but marked unchecked. This ladder, `W0 ≻ W1 ≻ W2 ≻ W3`, is the spine everything downstream builds on: nothing is graded by how convincing it sounds, only by how it could be checked again.

`src/ztare/scenarios/strength.py` uses that ladder to compute a graded strength per claim over the same graph the crisp verdict resolves. The semantics is the Quadratic Energy Model (Potyka, KR 2018), a damped forward-Euler iteration chosen over DF-QuAD (acyclic-only; ZTARE's maps have `CONTRADICTS` 2-cycles) and the h-categoriser (attack-only; the graph is bipolar). The moat-preserving choice is running QEM four times over nested strata instead of mapping warrant classes to numbers: stratum `k` keeps only edges at least as checkable as tier `k`, so `k=0` trusts only `W0` edges and `k=3` trusts everything. The output is a strength profile `(s0, s1, s2, s3)` — strength if you trust only kernel certificates, then add re-executable computation, then quotes, then proposals — built entirely from the warrant partial order (a leximax / System-Z-style stratification), with zero free parameters. `(0, 0, 0.97, 0.97)` reads as a castle of quotes: well supported, but nothing kernel-hard. Base scores are prior-free too: leaf evidence scores 1, every internal claim scores 0, so an unsupported challenge cannot drag down a well-evidenced thesis until it is itself evidence-backed. Support also collapses per provenance source — max within a source, sum across sources and across a source's `DERIVED_FROM` lineage — so fifty quotes from one document or its citation cascade cannot saturate a stratum the way independent corroboration does.

A crisp verdict can still override the number: the override lattice runs `REFUTED` (a surviving `W0`/`W1` attack on the thesis) ahead of `NONCONVERGENT`, ahead of `UNSUPPORTED` (no support at any tier), ahead of `CONTESTED` (show the profile — the status of essentially every live research map). Arithmetic never launders a kernel-grade refutation into a strength number.

Two consumers turn the profile into action. `shapley_support` runs exact removal-Shapley over the evidence sources — the characteristic function is thesis strength given only that subset present — so each source's contribution sums exactly to the thesis strength, naming what the decision rests on. The wager's `strength_displacement` (`src/ztare/scenarios/wager.py`) is the graded twin: it recomputes the profile under each of a wager's declared outcomes and ranks candidate experiments by the worst-case (maximin) movement across outcomes — "what would change my mind," asked of the graded number rather than only the crisp flip. Both are prior-free: Shapley needs no assigned weights, and worst-case ranking needs no outcome probabilities.

Determinism is unconditional: the QEM update is Lipschitz, so the iteration is unique and bit-reproducible. Convergence to a fixed point is proven in the literature only for acyclic graphs; no universal theorem covers cyclic bipolar semantics, so a run that hits its iteration cap is surfaced as `NONCONVERGENT` — an honest state, not a fabricated number. Cycle resistance is separately a theorem: the squashing function `h(x) = max(0,x)²/(1+max(0,x)²)` satisfies `h(x) < x` on `(0,1]`, so a pure support cycle with zero internal base weight decays to zero rather than self-lifting. The module's own test battery exercises the shapes this design exists to resist, as executable assertions: a `CONTRADICTS` 2-cycle between evidence-backed claims still converges under damping; two supports from one source collapse to the stronger, so one loud source cannot outscore independent corroboration; three claims `DERIVED_FROM` one root collapse to that root's single contribution rather than tripling it. That is representational validation, not a predictive-accuracy claim on live data — it shows the semantics distinguishes states the crisp verdict conflates. A head-to-head against alternative aggregators runs in `src/ztare/experiments/epistemic_lift_experiment.py`: on four adversarial synthetic topologies it compares the full combination against a naive flat QEM (single stratum, plain-sum, no override) and a count/volume heuristic. A sycophantic flood — fifty `W3` supports against one `W0` attack — is admitted by both baselines but fires `REFUTED` under the override lattice; a `CONTRADICTS` 2-cycle converges under damping where the undamped iteration does not; a five-way `DERIVED_FROM` citation cascade collapses to one source's strength (0.5) where the naive sum reads 0.96; and on a clean two-source control all three agree, so the divergences are not a universal artifact. The value tested is adversarial resilience, not accuracy on live data. Three limits bound the result. The comparison baseline is the same semantics with the strata, source-collapse, and override removed, so each divergence isolates one mechanism rather than a bundle. The deadlock test uses a tightly coupled pair of mutual contradictions, because a lone contradiction pair already settles on its own — damping only earns its place once the loop is tight enough to otherwise oscillate. The language-model-judge comparison is run (codex gpt-5.5, one call per topology, env-gated behind `ZTARE_LIFT_LLM_JUDGE=1`) in three arms of increasing charity to the model: `cold` (the evidence as raw prose — the realistic paste-and-ask), `structured` (typed relations, checkability tiers hidden), and `labeled` (the typed relations plus the same warrant tiers the system extracts). On the sycophantic flood the model is fooled by volume in both the cold and the structured arm — fifty unverified supports over one decisive refutation read as supported, where the override holds `REFUTED` — and it holds only in the labeled arm, where it is handed the checkability tiers directly. On the citation cascade it reads supported in all three arms, even when the prose states plainly that all five sources derive from one: it does not discount derivative provenance, a discount only the lineage collapse applies. The thing that flips the flood verdict is exactly the structure the system surfaces deterministically and a prompt does not supply; the model reasons adequately once handed that structure but does not construct or enforce it. The comparison is illustrative — one call per synthetic topology — not a benchmark.

Every building block here is classical, and several are direct prior art to cite rather than claim. Shapley attribution over a Quadratic Energy Model already exists — over edges rather than sources — in Yin, Potyka and Toni (*Relation Attribution Explanations*, IJCAI 2024) and the impact-measures line (AAMAS 2025). Building a bipolar argumentation framework from model outputs and scoring it with a gradual semantics is the ArgLLMs line (Freedman, Rago, Potyka, Toni, 2024). Refining a result over ordered strata into a lexicographic profile is preferred subtheories / System-Z (Brewka, 1989) and stratified labelings (Thimm and Kern-Isberner, 2013); replacing cardinal weights with ordinal certainty levels is possibilistic argumentation (Alsinet, Chesñevar, Godo). A reviewer-grade prior-art pass found no published work doing the specific synthesis: a *continuous* gradual semantics (QEM) with cardinal edge weights removed and replaced by an external epistemic-*checkability* ordering of the edges, re-run over nested strata and kept as an *uncollapsed* lexicographic profile. That synthesis is the defensible novel core — not a new primitive (the nested-strata-to-profile skeleton is itself old) but a specific assembly no prior work combines. It should be presented as an assembly with its lineage named, not as inventing stratified argument strength; the former holds under review, the latter does not.
