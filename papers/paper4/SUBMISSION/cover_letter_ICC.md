I am submitting **"The Cognitive Firm: A Multidivisional-Form Architecture
for Recursive AI Governance"** for consideration at *Industrial and
Corporate Change*. The paper is a theoretical contribution that extends
the Chandlerian and evolutionary-economics traditions ICC was built around
to a new class of firms: those whose routines are executed by language
models under sustained optimization pressure rather than by humans under
bounded rationality.

The contribution has three parts. First, the paper formalizes the
Chandlerian co-location pathology, the same structural problem documented
in single-form firms whose revenue divisions reported on their own
performance, as the load-bearing failure mode of recursive AI systems
that share generation and evaluation in a single optimization surface.
Second, the paper develops a small formal model (Section 4.5) that
characterizes effective capability as raw capability discounted by an
agency-cost function, derives a crossover condition for when the
multidivisional architecture dominates the unitary one, gives comparative
statics across the five state variables, and connects the framework
explicitly to Holmström's informativeness principle and Tirole's
incomplete-contracts framework. Five propositions (P1-P5) consolidate
the theoretical claims into testable form. Third, the paper illustrates
the framework through one operated implementation, treating the system as
illustrative of the pattern rather than as empirical validation of it,
and is explicit about the recursive co-location critique that applies to
the paper's own author-as-operator structure (Section 1).

The paper engages directly with the journal's recent conversation on
AI in firms. Schrepel and Pentland (2025) document concentration dynamics
among AI foundation models at the between-firm level; the present paper
proposes a within-firm governance architecture that complements that
analysis. Hötte, Tarannum, Verendel, and Bennett (2025) measure AI
development concentration at the firm level; the M-Form architecture
proposed here is one candidate response to the governance gap that line
of work documents. The paper's grounding in Nelson and Winter's
evolutionary-economics framework, Dosi's paradigms, and Teece-Pisano-Shuen
dynamic capabilities is intentional: the paper extends rather than
displaces these traditions, treating the recursive-AI firm as a new
instance of the routine-and-selection-pressure framework rather than as
a separate phenomenon requiring new theory.

The paper is N=1 in its empirical content by design, not by accident.
I have made this explicit in Section 1 ("Honest scope of evidence")
rather than letting reviewers infer it. The contribution rests on the
theoretical framework's coherence and on whether independent groups
operating different recursive AI systems find it generative; the
implementation in Section 5 is one instantiation that satisfies the
framework's predictions in the regimes tested. Three structural responses
to the recursive-co-location-applied-to-the-paper-itself critique are
deployed (theoretical positioning, capability-neutral propositions,
narrowed recurrence claim).

Solo authorship is my structural choice. The paper's prose, framing,
and empirical instantiation are mine; faculty advisors at Harvard
Business School (named in the Acknowledgements) shaped the historical
vocabulary the paper uses but did not co-author the contribution. I am
aware that solo authorship by a non-faculty author is harder at any
peer-reviewed venue and have done my best to compensate through the
formal model's depth, the literature engagement, and the replication
package. A reviewer who finds the contribution promising but the
authorship structure concerning is welcome to recommend conditional
acceptance pending a co-author with an established publication record;
I would welcome that path.

A devastatingly easy replication package is available: cloning the
public repository and running `make verify-paper4` reproduces Section 5.6's
deterministic verifier results against frozen sha256-snapshotted
implementation files in under sixty seconds, confirming the closure-time
verdict on re-run. The replication package is described in detail in
the Data and Code Availability section.
