## 1. Approach name

**Ergodic blow-down of the vorticity semigroup**

## 2. The mechanism

First, a necessary correction: as stated, the conjecture cannot be true, because every constant vector field  
\[
u(t,x)\equiv U_0\in\mathbb R^3,\qquad p\equiv 0,
\]
is a bounded ancient mild solution. Thus the most plausible Liouville target is

\[
u \equiv \text{constant},
\]

or \(u\equiv 0\) after imposing a normalization such as vanishing spatial mean, decay, or fixing the Galilean frame.

The proposed mechanism attacks the corrected rigidity statement by working entirely at the vorticity level and trying to prove that every bounded ancient mild solution has identically zero vorticity:
\[
\omega=\nabla\times u \equiv 0.
\]
Since \(u\in L^\infty_{t,x}\), the vorticity equation is meaningful after mollification:
\[
\partial_t \omega - \Delta \omega + u\cdot\nabla \omega = \omega\cdot\nabla u.
\]
The new idea is to use a **large-scale ergodic blow-down** rather than backward uniqueness. For \(R\to\infty\), define parabolically rescaled fields
\[
u_R(t,x)=u(R^2t,Rx),\qquad \omega_R(t,x)=R\,\omega(R^2t,Rx).
\]
The velocity remains uniformly bounded, while the vorticity is formally scale-subcritical in averaged negative norms. One tries to show that any nonzero vorticity produces a nontrivial invariant defect measure in the blow-down limit, but that the limiting defect measure is simultaneously dissipated by the heat part and transported by a bounded divergence-free drift. The contradiction would arise from an “ancient stationary statistical enstrophy balance”: a nonzero recurrent vorticity packet cannot survive indefinitely under diffusion at arbitrarily large scales unless the stretching term supplies energy, but the stretching term disappears in the blow-down limit in a suitable negative Besov topology.

The central quantity would be a localized, scale-averaged vorticity energy:
\[
\mathcal E_R(t)
=
\frac1{R^3}\int_{\mathbb R^3}
|\omega(t,x)|^2 \phi(x/R)\,dx,
\]
or more realistically a mollified version
\[
\mathcal E_{R,\ell}(t)
=
\frac1{R^3}\int |\omega_\ell(t,x)|^2\phi(x/R)\,dx,
\qquad \omega_\ell=\rho_\ell*\omega.
\]
The desired monotonicity is not pointwise in time but **ergodic in ancient time**:
\[
\limsup_{T,R\to\infty}
\frac1T\int_{-T}^0 \mathcal E_{R,\ell}(t)\,dt
\]
should vanish after sending \(R\to\infty\), then \(\ell\downarrow0\). If nonzero vorticity exists somewhere, parabolic unique continuation/local smoothing should force a positive lower density of vorticity energy across large ancient cylinders, contradicting this vanishing averaged enstrophy.

## 3. Why this is NEW

This is not the KNSŠ / ESS template. It does **not** try to apply backward uniqueness to conclude that vorticity vanishes from a terminal zero set. It also does **not** use an axisymmetric scalar maximum principle such as the equation for \(\omega_\theta/r\). The mechanism is instead statistical and large-scale: prove that bounded ancient solutions cannot carry a nonzero invariant vorticity defect measure under parabolic blow-down.

The new ingredient is an **ergodic compactness principle for ancient vorticity**, closer in spirit to homogenization, statistical hydrodynamics, or Furstenberg-type recurrence than to Carleman estimates. The hope is to sidestep the missing scalar maximum principle by not trying to control pointwise vorticity. Instead, one controls large-scale averaged vorticity in negative or mollified norms, where the vortex-stretching term may become perturbative under blow-down.

The classical obstacle is the term
\[
\omega\cdot\nabla u,
\]
which prevents a maximum principle for \(|\omega|\). The proposed sidestep is to observe that \(\nabla u\) is a Calderón-Zygmund transform of \(\omega\), and under large-scale averaging the bilinear stretching contribution may have zero density unless there is a nontrivial stationary vorticity cascade. The approach would try to rule out such a cascade by combining ancientness, bounded velocity, and local parabolic smoothing.

## 4. Three specific lemmas / sub-targets

### Lemma 1: Large-scale averaged vorticity dissipation

Let \(u\in L^\infty_{t,x}(\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3)\) be an ancient mild solution and \(\omega=\nabla\times u\) in distributions. For every standard mollifier \(\rho_\ell\), set \(\omega_\ell=\rho_\ell*\omega\). Prove that for every \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_2)\), \(\phi\equiv1\) on \(B_1\), \(0\leq\phi\leq1\),

\[
\limsup_{R\to\infty}
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\int_{-T}^0
\frac1{R^3}\int_{\mathbb R^3}
|\nabla \omega_\ell(t,x)|^2\phi(x/R)\,dx\,dt
=0
\]

after subsequently sending \(\ell\downarrow0\), or at least with a bound tending to zero like \(o_\ell(1)\).

A weaker but still useful target:

\[
\frac1T\int_{-T}^0
\|\omega_\ell(t)\|_{\dot H^{-1}(B_R)}^2\,dt
=o(R^3)
\quad\text{as }R,T\to\infty.
\]

The key difficulty is controlling the stretching term only from \(u\in L^\infty\).

---

### Lemma 2: Stretching has zero density in negative Besov norm

For bounded ancient mild solutions, prove an estimate of the form

\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\limsup_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\int_{-T}^0
\left|
\frac1{R^3}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}
(\omega_\ell\cdot\nabla u_\ell)\cdot \omega_\ell
\,\phi(x/R)\,dx
\right|dt
=0
\]

for fixed \(\ell>0\), or more realistically,

\[
\left|
\int
(\omega_\ell\cdot\nabla u_\ell)\cdot \omega_\ell \,\phi_R
\right|
\lesssim
\varepsilon(R,\ell)
\int |\nabla\omega_\ell|^2\phi_R
+
C_{\ell,M} o(R^3),
\]

with \(\varepsilon(R,\ell)\to0\) as \(R\to\infty\) after appropriate averaging in time.

This would be the genuinely new analytic heart. One possible route is a commutator-cancellation identity using

\[
\nabla u = \nabla\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega,
\]

together with spatial averaging to show that the trilinear vortex-stretching form has no extensive component for ancient bounded flows.

---

### Lemma 3: Nonzero vorticity implies positive ancient density

Assume \(\omega(t_0,x_0)\neq0\) in the sense that for some \(\ell>0\),

\[
|\omega_\ell(t_0,x_0)|\geq c_0>0.
\]

Then prove a recurrence/lower-density statement:

\[
\liminf_{R\to\infty}
\liminf_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\int_{-T}^0
\frac1{R^3}\int_{B_R}
|\omega_\ell(t,x)|^2\,dx\,dt
\geq c(c_0,\ell,M)>0.
\]

This is the other hard piece. It would say that a single nonzero vorticity event in an ancient bounded solution cannot be isolated in spacetime; ancientness plus bounded drift forces it to recur with positive spacetime density. The proof might use Krylov-Safonov/Harnack-type estimates for the mollified vorticity magnitude, or a quantitative propagation-of-smallness theorem for the drift-diffusion-stretching system.

Together, Lemmas 1–2 would force zero large-scale vorticity density, while Lemma 3 would force positive density from any nonzero vorticity. Hence \(\omega\equiv0\). Then \(u\) is bounded, divergence-free, curl-free on \(\mathbb R^3\), so \(u(t,\cdot)\) is spatially constant. The equation then forces this constant to be time-independent up to a pressure gradient; with the usual mild formulation and no external forcing, \(u\equiv U_0\). A further normalization would be needed to conclude \(U_0=0\).

## 5. Self-criticism

The largest danger is that Lemma 3 may be false or secretly equivalent to the desired Liouville theorem. Nonzero coherent structures could, in principle, drift to spatial infinity as \(t\to-\infty\) with zero density in every fixed blow-down frame. Because the Navier-Stokes equations are translation invariant, one must be careful: a vorticity packet may avoid recurrence in the uncentered average. One might need to average over all spatial centers or work with an invariant mean, but that risks becoming vacuous.

Lemma 2 is also highly speculative. The vortex-stretching term is exactly the supercritical obstruction in 3D. Claiming that it has zero large-scale density may amount to assuming absence of an ancient enstrophy cascade, which is close to the conjecture itself. The proposed cancellation would need to be genuinely harmonic-analytic, not just a disguised regularity estimate.

Another issue: \(u\in L^\infty\) does not immediately give \(\omega\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}\) uniformly in ancient time. Local smoothing gives positive-time regularity from any time slice, but for an ancient solution one must avoid using estimates whose constants depend on an initial time sent to \(-\infty\). Any argument involving \(|\omega|^2\) must therefore be mollified or justified through mild regularization.

Finally, the original statement \(u\equiv0\) is false without fixing the constant Galilean mode. This approach can at best prove vorticity-free rigidity, hence \(u\) spatially constant. An additional assumption is necessary to kill the constant solution.

## 6. Lean sketch

```lean
import Mathlib.Analysis.Calculus.ContDiff.Basic
import Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Basic
import Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Integral.Bochner.Basic

/-!
A toy formalization of the elementary endpoint of the proposed strategy:

If a sufficiently smooth vector field on ℝ³ is both curl-free and divergence-free,
one wants to conclude it is locally a harmonic gradient.  The full analytic
Liouville step would then use boundedness to show the harmonic potential is affine,
hence the velocity is constant.

This is only a schematic auxiliary statement, not a proof of Navier-Stokes
Liouville rigidity.
-/

noncomputable section

open scoped RealInnerProductSpace

abbrev Vec3 := Fin 3 → ℝ

-- Placeholder for Euclidean space ℝ³ as functions Fin 3 → ℝ.
abbrev R3 := Fin 3 → ℝ

/-- A placeholder derivative of a vector field. -/
constant jacobian : (R3 → Vec3) → R3 → (Fin 3 → Fin 3 → ℝ)

/-- Placeholder divergence. -/
def div (u : R3 → Vec3) (x : R3) : ℝ :=
  ∑ i : Fin 3, jacobian u x i i

/-- Placeholder curl components in ℝ³. -/
def curl (u : R3 → Vec3) (x : R3) : Vec3 :=
  fun i =>
    match i with
    | ⟨0, _⟩ => jacobian u x 2 1 - jacobian u x 1 2
    | ⟨1, _⟩ => jacobian u x 0 2 - jacobian u x 2 0
    | ⟨2, _⟩ => jacobian u x 1 0 - jacobian u x 0 1

/--
Schematic Liouville endpoint: a bounded, smooth, curl-free velocity field on ℝ³
should be spatially constant.  In a real development this would follow from
Poincaré lemma + bounded harmonic-gradient Liouville theorem.
-/
axiom bounded_curlfree_divfree_constant
  (u : R3 → Vec3)
  (h_smooth : ContDiff ℝ ⊤ u)
  (h_bound : ∃ M : ℝ, ∀ x : R3, ‖u x‖ ≤ M)
  (h_curl : ∀ x : R3, curl u x = 0)
  (h_div : ∀ x : R3, div u x = 0) :
  ∃ c : Vec3, ∀ x : R3, u x = c

end
```
