# 1. IMMUTABLE GROUND TRUTH
- A euro-area central fiscal capacity should be large enough to provide meaningful macroeconomic stabilization, not just exist institutionally. [Strength: medium; Sources: S002]
- Existing euro-area cross-border stabilization is thin relative to a mature federation, smoothing about 20% of idiosyncratic shocks compared to roughly 75% across US states. [Strength: medium; Sources: S002]
- Second-generation automatic stabilizers are valuable because they can deliver larger and more rules-based responses than ordinary discretionary action in severe downturns. [Strength: medium; Sources: S003]
- Mature or more developed federal-style stabilization systems are associated with larger standing automatic stabilizers. [Strength: medium; Sources: S003]
- The US Constitution, federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties are the 'supreme Law of the Land' (Article VI). [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- US state judges are bound by federal law notwithstanding contrary state constitutions or laws. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- Article VI of the US Constitution makes federal law higher than state and local law. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- Primacy is a core principle of EU law developed through CJEU jurisprudence. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- The principle of EU law primacy is challenged or not applied in certain EU member states. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- National constitutional courts in a number of EU member states have refused to draw the consequences of CJEU judgments. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- Systemic challenges to EU primacy threaten the EU legal order and the uniformity/effectiveness of EU law. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- In existing federations (US, Canada, Australia), fiscal transfers largely operate through automatic stabilizers embedded in a central budget, primarily through federal taxes and transfers to individuals. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- In existing federations, fiscal smoothing does not mainly operate through discretionary transfers from the center to subnational state budgets. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- Article 31 of Germany's Basic Law states that federal law takes precedence over Land law. [Strength: high; Sources: S007]
- Germany's Basic Law allocates powers between the Federation and the Länder within an explicit constitutional hierarchy. [Strength: high; Sources: S007]

# 3. IDENTIFIED CONTRADICTIONS
- Legal Supremacy Entrenchment
  - Claim A: Legal supremacy in established federations (US, Germany) is textually entrenched in the primary constitutional document, making federal law explicitly supreme and binding on subnational courts. [Sources: S004, S007]
  - Claim B: Legal supremacy in the EU (primacy) is a core principle developed through CJEU jurisprudence, not a freestanding treaty article, and is actively challenged or not applied by national constitutional courts in some member states. [Sources: S005]
  - Why it matters: The difference in entrenchment mechanism (textual vs. jurisprudential) and the ongoing 'recurrent constitutional contestation' in the EU vs. the established hierarchy in federations is a crucial discriminator for the 'durable equilibrium' legal pillar. It affects the stability and predictability of the legal order and the vulnerability to political challenges.
- Nature of Federal Fiscal Stabilization
  - Claim A: Fiscal stabilization in established federations (US, Canada, Australia) largely operates through automatic stabilizers embedded in a central budget, primarily via federal taxes and transfers to individuals, not through discretionary transfers to subnational governments. [Sources: S006]
  - Claim B: The EU's current fiscal architecture relies heavily on discretionary or temporary responses to crises, rather than fully matching the scale and rules-based nature of standing automatic stabilizers found in more developed federal-style systems, despite observed effectiveness of EU automatic stabilizers during shocks like COVID-19. [Sources: S003, S002]
  - Why it matters: This contradiction highlights the distinction between ad hoc, discretionary crisis management and standing, automatic, rules-based shock absorption, which is central to determining if the EU possesses 'durable-equilibrium-grade' fiscal pillars. The presence or absence of a preauthorized, automatic mechanism for a significant share of shocks impacts long-term stability and resilience.
