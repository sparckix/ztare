Title: The Constitution of the United States: A Transcription (Article VI)
URL: https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript
Date: accessed 2026-04-09

Claim / relevance:
- This is the official constitutional comparator for a mature union with an explicit supremacy rule embedded at the founding.
- It grounds the contrast between a federal system with treaty-level constitutional supremacy and the EU's primacy doctrine, which is jurisprudential and contested rather than textually entrenched in the treaties.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Article VI states that the Constitution, federal laws made pursuant to it, and treaties are the "supreme Law of the Land".
- It also states that judges in every state are bound by that law notwithstanding contrary state constitutions or laws.
- National Archives' explanatory material states directly that Article VI makes federal law higher than state and local law.

Why this matters for the thesis:
- This source gives an external comparator for what a high-integration legal pillar looks like when supremacy is explicit, founding-level, and binding on subnational judges.
- It supports an observable legal proxy for Mode DE: supremacy is not merely inferred from case law but constitutionally entrenched in the primary constitutional text.
- It also strengthens the thesis's contrast with the EU, where primacy is real but not a freestanding treaty article and therefore more exposed to recurrent constitutional contestation.
