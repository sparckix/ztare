Title: Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany (Article 31)
URL: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html
Date: accessed 2026-04-09

Claim / relevance:
- This source gives a second mature-federation legal comparator beyond the United States.
- It is directly relevant to the unresolved legal pillar question around what a durable-equilibrium-grade supremacy arrangement looks like in a federal system.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Article 31 of Germany's Basic Law states plainly: federal law takes precedence over Land law.
- The Basic Law also allocates powers between the Federation and the Länder within an explicit constitutional hierarchy rather than relying on a jurisprudential primacy doctrine alone.
- This provides a founding-text, constitution-level supremacy rule inside an established federal system.
- Germany therefore offers a comparator where constitutional contestation can still exist, but the baseline supremacy hierarchy itself is textually entrenched rather than left to an inferred doctrine.

Why this matters for the thesis:
- This helps move the legal pillar away from a US-only benchmark.
- It supports a more defensible external pattern: durable federations can differ in politics and conflict frequency, but they tend to entrench a clear supremacy hierarchy in primary constitutional text.
- That strengthens the thesis's contrast with the EU, where primacy is real but still lacks a freestanding treaty article and remains vulnerable to recurrent apex-court reopening.
- It also helps answer the "other federations" objection by showing the Mode DE legal pillar is not being inferred from one special case alone.

- Basic Law English text, Article 31
- Adjacent Articles 30-32 for allocation of powers context
