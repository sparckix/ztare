Title: The implementation of the principle of primacy of EU law
URL: https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1764670
Date: 2023-11-07

Claim / relevance:
- This official European Parliament summary externally grounds the claim that EU-law primacy is not merely an abstract doctrine but an implementation problem that is challenged in some member states.
- It is directly relevant to the unresolved legal-pillar seam around "reduced recurrent constitutional contestation".

Key facts / excerpts:
- The report says primacy is a core principle of EU law developed through CJEU jurisprudence.
- It also says primacy is challenged or not applied in certain member states, even though the vast majority of courts apply it.
- The report recommends considering an explicit treaty provision on primacy.
- It notes that national constitutional courts have in a number of cases refused to draw the consequences of CJEU judgments.
- It states that systemic challenges to primacy threaten the legal order and the uniformity/effectiveness of EU law.

Why this matters for the thesis:
- This source helps externalize the legal pillar by showing that contested primacy is not a thesis-authored complaint; it is recognized by an EU institution as a live implementation and constitutional-order problem.
- It supports an observable legal proxy for Mode FI: recurrent or strategically significant constitutional challenges to primacy remain present enough that EU institutions are still discussing explicit treaty entrenchment and infringement responses.
- It also suggests a better legal discriminator than a purely numerical threshold: whether primacy is textually entrenched and ordinarily uncontested in apex-court practice, rather than repeatedly reopened as a constitutional question.
