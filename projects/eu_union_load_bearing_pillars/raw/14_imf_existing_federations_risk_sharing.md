Title: The Role of Fiscal Transfers in Smoothing Regional Shocks: Evidence from Existing Federations
URL: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2016/12/31/The-Role-of-Fiscal-Transfers-in-Smoothing-Regional-Shocks-Evidence-from-Existing-Federations-44111
Date: 2016-07-21

Claim / relevance:
- This source directly addresses the current blocking gap around whether durable federations rely mainly on discretionary resilience or on standing fiscal mechanisms.
- It is especially useful because it studies existing federations rather than euro-area hypotheticals.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The paper studies three large existing federations: the United States, Canada, and Australia.
- It finds that fiscal transfers offset roughly 4-11% of idiosyncratic regional shocks and 13-24% of permanent shocks.
- Crucially, it says this insurance "largely operates through automatic stabilizers embedded in a central budget", primarily through federal taxes and transfers to individuals.
- It also says this smoothing does not mainly operate through discretionary transfers from the center to subnational state budgets.
- The paper explicitly presents these results as relevant for euro-area fiscal risk-sharing design.

Why this matters for the thesis:
- This is the cleanest external-validation source yet for the thesis's claim that durable-equilibrium-style shock absorption in mature federations is not primarily a matter of repeated discretionary improvisation.
- It does not prove that every federation must look identical, but it materially weakens the rival claim that discretionary resilience is a normal or sufficient equilibrium benchmark for durable federations as a class.
- It gives the project a much stronger external basis for saying that standing, embedded, preauthorized mechanisms are characteristic of durable federations, while ad hoc crisis response is not the primary comparator pattern.
