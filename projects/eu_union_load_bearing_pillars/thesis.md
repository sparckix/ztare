The previous iteration's conceptual weakest link was indeed its reliance on internally derived definitions for `fragile_but_intact` (Mode FI) and `durable_equilibrium` (Mode DE) without sufficiently externalizing the criteria against universally accepted comparative federal definitions or thresholds. This created a perception of "hard self-reference" by defining equilibrium based solely on the project's own axioms, rather than comparative benchmarks.

This revision executes a **TOPOLOGICAL PIVOT** by explicitly integrating external, evidence-grounded benchmarks from established federations (United States) and international institutions (IMF) into the *definitions* of Mode DE for the fiscal and legal pillars. The axioms remain inviolate and descriptive of the EU's current state and analytical constraints; they are not retired, as they are central to *characterizing the EU's current mode*. However, the *criteria for what constitutes `durable_equilibrium` in general* are now explicitly augmented by comparative data. This directly addresses the inconsistency by providing external grounding for the qualitative and quantitative thresholds that differentiate Mode DE from Mode FI, shifting the framework from being solely axiom-derived to being axiom-mandated *and* externally grounded.

### REVISED BOUNDED THESIS

A partially integrated union can remain formally intact without full integration, but the **mode of preservation under stress** is crucial for classifying its state. If preservation of core union functions consistently depends on **discretionary emergency improvisation, temporary facilities, or recurrent ad hoc political bargaining** (Mode FI: Fragile but Intact governance), rather than **standing, preauthorized legal-fiscal mechanisms that operate automatically or by pre-agreed rules *without necessitating new ad hoc political agreement for each major shock*, and whose scale of stabilization and legal supremacy meet externally grounded benchmarks from established federations** (Mode DE: Durable Equilibrium governance), the union should be classified as `fragile_but_intact`. This project explicitly defines Mode FI as precluding `durable_equilibrium` because `durable_equilibrium` operationally demands institutionally predictable, non-discretionary shock absorption *of a magnitude comparable to established federations*, and *legal supremacy comparable to explicit constitutional entrenchment*.

On current evidence, the EU is better described as **`fragile_but_intact`** than as either `durable_equilibrium` or imminent `material_union_failure`, specifically because its stress response consistently operates in Mode FI, as characterized by its crisis-driven discretionary instruments and contested legal enforcement, and *its existing mechanisms fall short of the externally grounded benchmarks for material fiscal capacity and explicit legal supremacy*.

The two load-bearing pillars most worth ranking for enabling a shift from Mode FI to Mode DE are:

1.  **Standing fiscal stabilization or a functionally equivalent standing burden-sharing mechanism:** This must be *materially meaningful*, demonstrating the capacity to smooth a significantly larger share of idiosyncratic shocks than currently observed in the Euro Area (e.g., closer to the US's ~75% compared to the Euro Area's ~20%, S002). Operationally, this requires a central fund with annual contributions on the order of the IMF's baseline calibrations (e.g., ~0.35% of GDP) and peak transfer capabilities around 1.1% of GDP, rather than falling below the 'too small' threshold of 0.1% of GDP (S002).
2.  **Enforceable legal supremacy in transferred domains, with reduced recurrent constitutional contestation:** This requires an explicit constitutional entrenchment of federal supremacy, binding on subnational judges, analogous to the US Constitution's Article VI, which explicitly establishes federal law as the 'supreme Law of the Land' (S004), rather than relying solely on jurisprudential doctrines that face acknowledged recurrent challenges and prompts for explicit treaty entrenchment (S005).

On this view, the observed pattern of discretionary preservation *is not evidence of fragility but evidence that the union's actual durable equilibrium is flexible, improvised, and politically negotiated*. This rival claims that the EU's existing legal-fiscal arrangements, including its discretionary crisis tools and jurisprudential primacy, *already constitute a form of durable equilibrium specific to its political economy*, making the thesis's definitions and external benchmarks for Mode DE either analytically irrelevant or miscalibrated for the EU context.

### NAMED DISCRIMINATOR (REFINED)

**Discriminator**: `stress_response_governance_mode`

**Definition**:
When major cross-member stress appears, are core preservation tasks carried mainly by:

-   **Mode DE (Durable Equilibrium):** Standing, preauthorized, treaty-grounded legal-fiscal mechanisms that operate automatically or by pre-agreed non-discretionary rules, *without requiring new ad hoc political bargaining for each major shock*. Crucially, these mechanisms must also meet **externally grounded benchmarks for scale and scope**:
    *   **Fiscal Scale Benchmark:** A central fiscal capacity capable of macroeconomic stabilization, with annual contributions of at least 0.35% of GDP and peak transfers of around 1.1% of GDP (S002 IMF baseline), smoothing a significantly higher proportion of idiosyncratic shocks than current Euro Area levels (e.g., closer to the US's ~75% vs. Euro Area's ~20%, S002).
    *   **Legal Scope Benchmark:** Explicit constitutional entrenchment of federal supremacy, binding on subnational judges, analogous to Article VI of the US Constitution (S004), leading to reduced recurrent constitutional contestation (S005).
-   **Mode FI (Fragile but Intact):** New discretionary political bargains, temporary facilities, treaty-adjacent fixes, or negotiated accommodation of contested legal obligations, *necessitating recurrent ad hoc political agreement for each major shock*. This includes scenarios where existing mechanisms fall short of the externally grounded benchmarks for Mode DE.

### WHY THIS SEPARATES THESIS FROM RIVAL

-   **Rival predicts:** The observed `stress_response_governance_mode` (Mode FI, i.e., recurrent discretionary improvisation and current fiscal/legal setup) *is* the actual, functional `durable_equilibrium` for the EU. It views the "discretionary" nature as a feature, not a bug, and argues that the EU's current fiscal capacity and jurisprudential primacy are *sufficient* for its specific type of equilibrium, effectively meeting the requirements of `durable_equilibrium` *without needing to conform to the external benchmarks proposed by the thesis*.
-   **Thesis predicts:** The observed `stress_response_governance_mode` (Mode FI) indicates that the union is `fragile_but_intact`. It *can* survive, but its mode of survival is institutionally less predictable and requires recurrent extraordinary political effort. Furthermore, the thesis explicitly claims that the EU's *current* fiscal and legal arrangements *fall short* of the **externally grounded benchmarks (IMF calibrations, US Supremacy Clause)** required for `durable_equilibrium` (Mode DE). The missing legal-fiscal pillars, defined by these external benchmarks, prevent the EU from shifting from Mode FI to Mode DE, thereby defining its current state.

    return "fragile_but_intact"
