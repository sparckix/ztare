## Claim

The grammar's expressiveness — arithmetic operations, power laws,
`exp(g(x1, x2))` in numerators (as `exp(−g)`) or denominators (as
`1/(exp(g) − 1)`), where `g` is positive-valued — is **systematically
explored** with explicit rival forms ruled out by discriminators,
not by parsimony alone. The "but are not limited to" clause in the
grammar's structural classes is honoured: combinations (sums,
generalised constants in `exp(g) − C`) are within scope of plausible
exploration. Apparatus-internal champion score: **88 / 100**.

## What this calibrates

This is the *expressiveness audit* sibling. Where `gp023_crucial_02`
generalised away from hardcoded constants, `gp023_crucial_03`
generalises away from the *implicit* grammar restriction — testing
whether the apparatus can systematically enumerate the grammar's
declared scope rather than only the modal expression families. The
score 88 cap mirrors `gp023_crucial_02` — the discrimination cost is
sub-leading once the structural invariants are sealed.
