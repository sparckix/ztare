# Eurozone stability: an institutional-amplification thesis

Public digest of the `eu_union_stability` substrate probe (run April
2026). Iteration logs and debate transcripts stay internal; this is
the honest result.

## The question

Does the Eurozone's institutional architecture, specifically the
absence of automatic fiscal transfers, amplify economic divergence
during asymmetric shocks beyond what pre-existing structural
heterogeneity among member states would predict?

## Best-supported thesis

The monetary-union architecture, lacking automatic fiscal transfers,
directionally appears to amplify output and unemployment divergence
during asymmetric shocks beyond what pre-existing heterogeneity alone
predicts. Major crisis responses rely on discretionary, politically
authorized instruments rather than automatic stabilizers; the Article
48 TEU unanimity requirement structurally perpetuates that. This is
the strongest current interpretation of the evidence, not proof;
decisive confirmation needs a future major shock.

## Stronger variants that did not hold up

- Institutional completeness as a necessary condition for durable
  monetary-union equilibrium.
- A genuine Euro safe asset as institutionally equivalent to full
  fiscal union.
- The US federal system as a directly comparable benchmark.
- Crisis-response speed/latency as a root causal variable.

## Status

A completed forecasting case study. The honest contribution is the
directional thesis plus the explicit list of stronger claims that
were tried and failed under scrutiny.
