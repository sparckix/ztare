# 3. IDENTIFIED CONTRADICTIONS
- cache defect as independent root cause vs. symptom/amplifier
  - Claim A: Cache miss rates amplify as a consequence of export lag rather than acting as an independent root cause. [Sources: S007, S009]
  - Claim B: A future row showing high cache misses during healthy exports would reopen the cache-defect hypothesis. [Sources: S009]
  - Why it matters: Determines whether the support-console cache is an independent failure mode or merely a downstream effect of export failures, which changes remediation priorities.
- causal mechanism for duplicate_payment_warning and downstream_console_index_missing errors
  - Claim A: The two error codes (duplicate_payment_warning and downstream_console_index_missing) share a common root cause: a lease conflict on billing_status_index caused by CHG-142's deferred index commit. [Sources: S010]
  - Claim B: The two error codes may represent independent failures; batch b-7103 first retried then failed with duplicate_payment_warning, while other batches failed with downstream_console_index_missing. [Sources: S005]
  - Why it matters: If errors share a common mechanism, fixing one (rollback of CHG-142) resolves both; if independent, separate root cause analysis is needed for each error type.
- applicability of synthetic evidence to real operations
  - Claim A: The local verifier weakens the independent-cache-defect hypothesis for this synthetic dataset. [Sources: S009]
  - Claim B: The note does not claim the same mechanism applies outside the synthetic operations dataset. [Sources: S010, S009]
  - Why it matters: Whether findings from this synthetic fixture can be generalized to production environments directly affects the validity of any real-world remediation plan.
- relative explanatory power of CHG-143 vs. CHG-142
  - Claim A: CHG-143 (timeout tolerance change) is weaker as a root-cause explanation than the rolled-back export batching flag (CHG-142). [Sources: S007]
  - Claim B: CHG-143 (support-console read-timeout increase) may have contributed to or masked the backlog. [Sources: S001]
  - Why it matters: If CHG-143 contributed significantly, reverting it could be part of the solution; if not, focus should remain on the batching flag.
