# 1. IMMUTABLE GROUND TRUTH
- Primacy is a core principle of EU law developed through CJEU jurisprudence. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- National constitutional courts have, in a number of cases, refused to draw the consequences of CJEU judgments. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- A standard definition of European disintegration is still lacking as of 2019. [Strength: high; Sources: S013]
- The paper studies three existing federations: the United States, Canada, and Australia. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- Fiscal transfers offset roughly 4-11% of idiosyncratic regional shocks in these federations. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- Fiscal transfers offset roughly 13-24% of permanent shocks in these federations. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- ECB staff estimate the euro-area aggregate automatic fiscal stabilizer at 0.48 using the ESCB semi-elasticity approach. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- The European Commission estimate for the euro-area aggregate automatic fiscal stabilizer is 0.56. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- The paper's central result is that monetary accommodation alone can sustain the union only for a while, not permanently. [Strength: high; Sources: S008]
- The paper's logic implies that central-bank action can buy time, but a sequence of large asymmetric shocks can still eventually break the union if fiscal transfer capacity is absent. [Strength: high; Sources: S008]
- The paper examines the Latin Monetary Union, the Scandinavian Monetary Union, and the Austro-Hungarian Monetary Union. [Strength: high; Sources: S009]
- The three early monetary unions could not withstand the shock of World War I. [Strength: high; Sources: S009]
- Eichengreen argued in 2007 that one or more members leaving the euro area in the next ten years was unlikely. [Strength: high; Sources: S011]
- Eichengreen argued in 2007 that total disintegration of the euro area was even more unlikely. [Strength: high; Sources: S011]
- Euro-area sovereign spread movements during the crisis were an episode of unusually high volatility. [Strength: high; Sources: S016]
- Spread movements were first driven strongly by common global risk repricing and then increasingly by country-specific solvency concerns. [Strength: high; Sources: S016]
- Differentiated integration/disintegration is a central legal and political feature of the EU after the euro crisis and Brexit. [Strength: high; Sources: S019]
- Substantial variation, opt-outs, and differentiated participation can coexist with continuing formal Union membership and a still-functioning legal order. [Strength: high; Sources: S019]
- The modern EMU has a materially different and more developed resilience architecture than earlier monetary unions. [Strength: high; Sources: S020]
- Fragmentation and redenomination risk are state-dependent and responsive to policy announcements. [Strength: high; Sources: S018]

# 3. IDENTIFIED CONTRADICTIONS
- transferability_of_historical_union_failure_rates
  - Claim A: The historical failure rate (3/3) of pre-WWI monetary unions provides a defensible upper bound for EMU failure probability. [Sources: S009]
  - Claim B: Historical monetary union failure episodes are not quantitatively transferable to the modern EU as direct rate anchors; they are upper-tail comparators requiring discounting for modern institutional resilience. [Sources: S020, S017]
  - Why it matters: Directly affects the base failure probability prior: one side suggests a high prior from historical data, the other argues such a transfer would be invalid without a large discount.
- stability_of_intermediate_states
  - Claim A: Differentiation alone does not constitute material union failure; durable legal/institutional rupture is required. [Sources: S019]
  - Claim B: The most stable post-Brexit outcome may be an indeterminate arrangement rather than either pole, implying intermediate states can be sticky. [Sources: S015]
  - Why it matters: If intermediate states are stable, the binary failure event may require a multi-state model rather than a simple probability of one terminal event.
- cushioning_effect_vs_GDP_deficit_contribution
  - Claim A: ECB model simulations indicate euro-area automatic stabilizers cushion about 10% to 30% of a standard GDP shock. [Sources: S005]
  - Claim B: During the COVID-19 crisis, automatic stabilizers were expected to account for about 2.8% of GDP out of an 8.5% GDP deficit in 2020 (approx. 33% of the deficit). [Sources: S005]
  - Why it matters: The two metrics (shock cushioning vs. deficit contribution) could suggest different magnitudes; resolving the discrepancy is needed for calibrating the survival mechanism term.

# 4. EPISTEMIC VOIDS (OPEN PROBLEMS / UNKNOWNS)
- What exactly counts as 'material union failure by 2035-01-01'?
  - Why it matters: The entire probability estimate depends on a settled event ontology; without it, all evidence collection and modeling are misaligned.
  - Blocking effect: All downstream modeling, probability assignment, and scenario classification.
- What reference class justifies the base failure probability?
  - Why it matters: Determines whether the prior is empirically grounded or arbitrary.
  - Blocking effect: Base_failure_probability assignment.
- Whether discretionary resilience constitutes a durable equilibrium or whether the current EU status is genuinely fragile.
  - Why it matters: Determines the direction and magnitude of survival mechanism adjustments in the model.
  - Blocking effect: Scenario construction and classification of EU trajectory.
- The exact numerical value of stress_escalation_multiplier for 2035 scenarios.
  - Why it matters: The stress term is identified as the dominant variance driver in the latest model run.
  - Blocking effect: Calibrating the upward trigger channel in failure models.
- Whether the euro-area aggregate stabilizer estimate applies uniformly across all member states or masks significant heterogeneity.
  - Why it matters: Failure probability may be driven by asymmetric shocks hitting weaker member states with smaller stabilizers.
  - Blocking effect: Assessing whether the aggregate cushioning is sufficient to prevent a member-state-level failure event.
- Which specific policy announcements or institutional responses reduce fragmentation risk and by how much.
  - Why it matters: Affects the magnitude and timing of hazard rate changes over the 2007-2035 window.
  - Blocking effect: Calibrating a time-varying hazard model.
