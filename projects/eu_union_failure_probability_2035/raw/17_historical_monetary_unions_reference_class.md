Title: Lessons from historical monetary unions - is the European monetary union making the same mistakes?
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10368-018-0416-8
Date: 2018-04-04

Claim / relevance:
- This source helps with the base-rate / reference-class problem by giving a compact comparative frame for historical monetary unions and what made them fragile or durable.
- It is useful because the probabilistic project needs a defensible comparison set rather than an invented `base_failure_probability`.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The paper examines three historical monetary unions: the Latin Monetary Union, the Scandinavian Monetary Union, and the Austro-Hungarian Monetary Union.
- It argues that robust common institutions, including a common central bank and unified fiscal policy, are important for withstanding external shocks.
- It says the three early unions could not withstand the shock of World War I.
- It also argues that continuing national rivalries can undermine a monetary union.
- The paper frames these cases explicitly as lessons for EMU rather than as identical analogues, which makes them usable as a bounded reference class rather than as a one-to-one prediction template.

Why this matters for probability:
- This does not give a final numerical prior by itself, but it narrows the lawful reference-class space.
- It supports a probability model in which missing common fiscal institutions and unresolved political rivalries keep failure risk above zero.
- It also warns against using only success cases or only EU-specific narratives when setting the prior.
