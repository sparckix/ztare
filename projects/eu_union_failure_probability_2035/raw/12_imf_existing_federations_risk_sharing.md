Title: The Role of Fiscal Transfers in Smoothing Regional Shocks: Evidence from Existing Federations
URL: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2016/12/31/The-Role-of-Fiscal-Transfers-in-Smoothing-Regional-Shocks-Evidence-from-Existing-Federations-44111
Date: 2016-07-21

Claim / relevance:
- This source is relevant to the probability project because it constrains how much protective weight can be assigned to standing fiscal risk-sharing in mature federations.
- It helps separate true automatic shock absorption from discretionary crisis response when thinking about survival probabilities through 2035.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The paper studies three existing federations: the United States, Canada, and Australia.
- It finds that fiscal transfers offset roughly 4-11% of idiosyncratic regional shocks and 13-24% of permanent shocks.
- The insurance largely operates through automatic stabilizers embedded in a central budget, especially federal taxes and transfers to individuals.
- The paper says this smoothing does not mainly operate through discretionary transfers from the center to subnational budgets.
- The paper presents the results as relevant for euro-area fiscal risk-sharing design.

Why this matters for probability:
- This is evidence that durable survival in mature federations is supported by embedded, standing mechanisms rather than mainly by repeated improvisation.
- In the probability model, it supports treating weak standing fiscal risk-sharing as a factor that should keep failure risk above the floor, rather than assuming discretionary rescue fully neutralizes tail risk.
- It also constrains the `discretionary_backstop_reduction` term: ad hoc preservation may reduce failure probability, but this source argues that mature-federation resilience is not built primarily on ad hoc transfers.
