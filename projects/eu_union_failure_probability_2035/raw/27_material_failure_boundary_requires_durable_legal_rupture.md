Title: Differentiated disintegration is not automatically material union failure
URL: https://academic.oup.com/jiel/article/23/2/489/5874723
Date: 2020-07-30

Claim / relevance:
- This source directly helps with the live blocking gap on `material_union_failure_definition`.
- It matters because the current project still needs a defensible boundary between:
  - severe but still formally intact differentiated integration/disintegration
  - and `material_union_failure` as a probability target by 2035.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Markakis argues that differentiated integration/disintegration in the EU is not a peripheral anomaly but a central legal and political feature of the Union after the euro crisis and Brexit.
- The article stresses that legal and institutional arrangements matter, not just whether some policy areas move to an "outer core."
- It explicitly argues that future arrangements may differ little from flexibility and variation that already exist within parts of the EU.
- In other words: substantial variation, opt-outs, and differentiated participation can coexist with continuing formal Union membership and a still-functioning legal order.
- Related comparative work on differentiated integration after Brexit identifies multiple empirical models of differentiation inside and outside the Union rather than a single collapse pathway:
  - https://doi.org/10.1111/jcms.12957

Why this matters for probability:
- This is strong support for a narrower event boundary.
- It weakens any model that treats:
  - multi-speed integration,
  - selective opt-outs,
  - or outer-core arrangements
  as automatic `material_union_failure`.
- The defensible modeling move is to reserve `material_union_failure` for a durable rupture of core legal/institutional intactness, not for differentiation alone.
- Inference from the cited literature:
  - the binary target should likely hinge on durable exit, durable euro-area breakup/reversibility, or a comparably deep legal-institutional rupture
  - while reversible or treaty-contained differentiation should remain on the `formal_intactness` side of the boundary
