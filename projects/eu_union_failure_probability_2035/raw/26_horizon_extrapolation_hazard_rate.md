Title: Monetary policy, fragmentation risks and the euro
URL: https://www.banque-france.fr/en/publications-and-statistics/publications/monetary-policy-fragmentation-risks-and-euro
Date: 2021-11-04

Claim / relevance:
- This source helps with the unjustified 10-year-to-28-year extrapolation problem.
- The current `%` thesis still leans on a 2007 ten-year euro-breakup assessment while trying to speak about 2035. This source is useful because it shows fragmentation and redenomination risk are time-varying and policy-sensitive, not a constant hazard.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The Banque de France note argues that ECB policy affects the euro not only through rates but also through sovereign spreads, especially when redenomination risk is high.
- It says this relationship is particularly visible when fragmentation risk is elevated.
- It treats fragmentation and redenomination risk as state-dependent and responsive to policy announcements, not as a stable background constant.
- That matters because a 2007 judgment about the next ten years cannot simply be stretched to 2035 unless a hazard-rate assumption is made explicit and defended.

Why this matters for probability:
- It supports the firing squad's point that the horizon extrapolation in the current model is under-argued.
- A model that spans 2007-2035 needs to say whether hazard is:
  - constant,
  - front-loaded around major crisis episodes,
  - or reduced by later institutional responses.
- This source does not solve the hazard function by itself, but it gives a concrete reason why implicit constant-hazard extrapolation is not defensible.
