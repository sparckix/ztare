Title: Automatic fiscal stabilisers in the euro area and the COVID-19 crisis
URL: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/articles/2020/html/ecb.ebart202006_03~3175750a6d.en.html
Date: 2020-06

Claim / relevance:
- This source matters because the probability project needs evidence for the survival side, not only the failure side.
- It gives concrete magnitudes for the euro area's existing automatic stabilizers and therefore informs how much crisis cushioning is already present.

Key facts / excerpts:
- ECB staff estimate the euro-area aggregate automatic fiscal stabilizer at 0.48 using the ESCB semi-elasticity approach; the European Commission estimate is 0.56.
- ECB model simulations say euro-area automatic stabilizers cushion about 10% to 30% of a standard GDP shock.
- During the COVID-19 crisis, automatic stabilizers were expected to account for about 2.8% of GDP out of an 8.5% GDP deficit in 2020, rising to 5.2% of GDP cumulatively over 2020-21.
- The article notes that second-generation automatic stabilizers are valuable because they can deliver larger and more rules-based responses than ordinary discretionary action in severe downturns.

Why this matters for probability:
- This source argues against a collapse narrative that ignores existing shock absorbers.
- In the starter model, it supports a meaningful `discretionary_backstop_reduction` or broader survival-mechanism term rather than treating failure as a straight-line function of missing pillars.
- It also helps keep the probability object balanced: the EU may lack durable-equilibrium-grade institutions while still having enough stabilization to materially lower near-horizon failure odds.
