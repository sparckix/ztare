Title: Brexit: differentiated disintegration in the European Union
URL: https://doi.org/10.1080/13501763.2018.1467954
Date: 2018-04-25

Claim / relevance:
- This source directly supplies a more granular external taxonomy of disintegration.
- It matters because the probabilistic project currently needs a principled way to distinguish selective rollback, external differentiation, and true failure of formal intactness.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Schimmelfennig defines differentiated disintegration as the selective reduction of a state's level and scope of integration.
- The article distinguishes:
  - internal differentiation, where a member state remains in the EU but exits specific policies
  - external differentiation, where it exits the EU but continues participating in selected EU policies
- The abstract emphasizes that disintegration demands are often moderated in negotiation rather than moving directly to maximal rupture.

Why this matters for probability:
- This is one of the cleanest ontology sources for the project.
- It shows that not all disintegration is identical and not all rollback implies immediate destruction of formal intactness.
- It supports a narrower event boundary in which:
  - selective policy rollback or partial differentiation is not automatically `material_union_failure`
  - but full exit with durable legal-institutional rupture might be.
- It should help the thesis replace vague `equivalent failure state` language with a more defensible taxonomy.
