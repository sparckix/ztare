Title: The implementation of the principle of primacy of EU law
URL: https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1764670
Date: 2023-11-07

Claim / relevance:
- This source matters because the probability project needs a live legal-rupture channel, not just a fiscal fragility channel.
- It helps ground the idea that legal contestation is a real stress amplifier that could raise failure probability under adverse scenarios.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The report says primacy is a core principle of EU law developed through CJEU jurisprudence.
- It also says primacy is challenged or not applied in certain member states, even though the vast majority of courts apply it.
- The report recommends considering an explicit treaty provision on primacy.
- It notes that national constitutional courts have in a number of cases refused to draw the consequences of CJEU judgments.
- It states that systemic challenges to primacy threaten the legal order and the uniformity and effectiveness of EU law.

Why this matters for probability:
- This is evidence for a non-zero `legal_break_multiplier`: legal rupture risk is not imaginary or thesis-authored.
- At the same time, the source does not imply imminent breakdown, because it also says the vast majority of courts still apply primacy.
- That makes it useful for a bounded probability project: the legal channel should raise risk relative to a clean federal hierarchy, but not automatically imply near-term failure.
