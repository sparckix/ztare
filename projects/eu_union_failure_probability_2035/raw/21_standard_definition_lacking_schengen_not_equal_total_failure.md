Title: European disintegration: a new feature of EU politics
URL: https://aei.pitt.edu/100445/1/schramm_cepob_3-19.pdf
Date: 2019-05

Claim / relevance:
- This source is relevant because it says a standard definition of European disintegration is still lacking and distinguishes between different crisis episodes that did and did not display disintegrative features.
- It directly helps the project avoid collapsing every serious crisis into `material_union_failure`.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The brief says a standard definition, let alone a theory, of European disintegration is still lacking.
- It states that while disintegration hardly unfolded during the Eurozone crisis, the Schengen crisis displayed considerable disintegrative features.
- It treats Brexit as the clearest sign of European disintegration.
- It also says disintegration entails high political risks and potentially uncontrollable dynamics.

Why this matters for probability:
- This is useful negative evidence against a crude binary ontology.
- It shows that there can be disintegrative features without full union failure.
- For this project, that means the event boundary must distinguish:
  - serious but reversible disintegration in a policy field
  - from `material_union_failure` as a durable rupture of formal intactness.
- It also supports a modeling rule: not every observed disintegrative episode should mechanically increase `P(material_union_failure)` by the same amount.
