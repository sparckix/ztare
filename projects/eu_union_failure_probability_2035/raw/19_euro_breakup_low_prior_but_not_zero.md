Title: The Breakup of the Euro Area
URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w13393
Date: 2007-09

Claim / relevance:
- This source is useful on the downward side of the prior: it gives a serious pre-crisis scholarly argument that euro-area exit or total disintegration was unlikely over a ten-year horizon and would face formidable technical and political costs.
- It helps prevent the base failure probability from being set too high just because the directional project found institutional fragility.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Eichengreen argues that one or more members leaving the euro area in the next ten years was unlikely, and total disintegration even more unlikely.
- He also argues that the technical difficulties of reintroducing a national currency would be formidable.
- The paper adds that abandoning the euro was not certain to materially improve the economic problems of participating states and could carry longer-term political costs.

Why this matters for probability:
- This does not settle the 2035 question, and it predates later crises, so it should not dominate the model.
- But it is useful as a counterweight against exaggerated collapse priors.
- It supports a bounded prior in which failure probability is clearly non-zero but not treated as the modal outcome absent a strong new rupture channel.
