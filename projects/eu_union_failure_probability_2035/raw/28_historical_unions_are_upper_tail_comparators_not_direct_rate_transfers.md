Title: Historical monetary unions are upper-tail comparators, not direct rate-transfer anchors for the modern EU
URL: https://www.econstor.eu/handle/10419/173124
Date: 2017-10-26

Claim / relevance:
- This source directly targets the current degrading weakest link: whether historical monetary union failure episodes are quantitatively transferable to the modern EU for the `Severe Systemic Shock` multiplier.
- It matters because the latest `83` result says the stress multiplier is now the main live seam, and the firing squad is specifically attacking direct comparability.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Claeys argues that the euro crisis revealed major flaws in the euro architecture, but also that substantial reforms were put in place during and after the crisis.
- The paper's central point is not "the euro area is safe"; it is that the modern EMU has a materially different and more developed resilience architecture than earlier monetary unions, while still retaining important fragilities.
- The missing pieces are described in terms of remaining needs:
  - stronger banking union / financing union
  - better macroeconomic policy framework
  - clearer ESM / OMT governance
  - a euro-area stabilisation capacity
- ECB policy after the euro crisis and pandemic adds another important difference from pre-WWI monetary unions:
  - the Transmission Protection Instrument can be activated against unwarranted, disorderly market dynamics that threaten monetary-policy transmission across the euro area
  - https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2022/html/ecb.pr220721~973e6e7273.en.html

Why this matters for probability:
- Historical monetary unions still matter as a severe-shock cautionary reference class.
- But these sources support a stricter rule:
  - historical union failures are not cleanly transferable failure-rate anchors for the modern EU
  - they are better treated as upper-tail comparators whose implications must be discounted through the modern EU's institutional resilience channels
- Inference from the cited sources:
  - the `[1.30, 1.70]` severe-shock multiplier should not be defended as a near-direct transfer from pre-WWI monetary union collapse episodes
  - it is more defensible as a comparator-bounded stress range that is explicitly adjusted downward for today's ECB anti-fragmentation tools, crisis backstops, and post-2012 architecture
- This does not eliminate the severe-shock channel.
- It does mean the thesis should justify the quantitative transfer with an explicit comparability discount rather than by historical analogy alone.
