Title: Managing differentiated disintegration: Insights from comparative federalism on post-Brexit EU-UK relations
URL: https://doi.org/10.1177/1369148120968516
Date: 2020-11-16

Claim / relevance:
- This source helps the ontology seam by showing that institutional arrangements can occupy relatively stable intermediate positions rather than sorting cleanly into full integration or total breakdown.
- It is relevant because the firing squad is challenging whether the project's binary complement really captures the space of plausible intermediate states.

Key facts / excerpts:
- The article studies a spectrum of post-Brexit outcomes rather than a single rupture model.
- It uses comparative federalism and identifies four safeguards: structural, judicial, partisan, and socio-cultural.
- It concludes that the most stable post-Brexit outcome may be an indeterminate arrangement rather than either pole.
- It explicitly discusses a range from closer institutionalized alignment to total breakdown of common obligations.

Why this matters for probability:
- This is evidence that intermediate institutional states can be durable enough to matter analytically.
- For the EU probability project, it strengthens the need to specify why some ambiguous or degraded states still count as `formal_intactness` rather than `material_union_failure`.
- It also suggests that a future probability model may need to track state families first, then collapse them into the binary event only at the final reporting layer.
