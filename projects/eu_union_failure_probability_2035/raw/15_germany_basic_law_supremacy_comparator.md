Title: Basic Law for the Federal Republic of Germany (Article 31)
URL: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html
Date: accessed 2026-04-09

Claim / relevance:
- This source provides a mature-federation legal comparator for what a textually entrenched supremacy hierarchy looks like.
- It helps the probability project distinguish ordinary constitutional conflict from deeper hierarchy instability.

Key facts / excerpts:
- Article 31 of Germany's Basic Law states that federal law takes precedence over Land law.
- The Basic Law allocates powers between the Federation and the Länder within an explicit constitutional hierarchy.
- The comparator therefore shows a federal system where supremacy is anchored in primary constitutional text rather than resting only on jurisprudential doctrine.

Why this matters for probability:
- This supports treating the EU's legal-order contestation as a real fragility channel relative to a more textually entrenched federal baseline.
- It does not directly give a point probability, but it constrains the probability model by showing what a lower-legal-risk comparator looks like.
- It can therefore inform adverse and non-adverse legal scenarios rather than functioning as a one-shot verdict on EU failure.
