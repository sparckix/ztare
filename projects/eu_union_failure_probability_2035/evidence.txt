EU_UNION_FAILURE_PROBABILITY_2035 — COMPILED EVIDENCE (April 11, 2026)

This file is a structured evidence brief for adversarial validation.
It preserves contradictions and unknowns instead of smoothing them away.

# 1. IMMUTABLE GROUND TRUTH
- The euro area's aggregate automatic fiscal stabilizer is estimated at 0.48 (ESCB semi-elasticity approach) or 0.56 (European Commission estimate). [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- Euro-area automatic stabilizers cushion approximately 10% to 30% of a standard GDP shock according to ECB model simulations. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- During COVID-19, euro-area automatic stabilizers were expected to account for approximately 2.8% of GDP out of an 8.5% GDP deficit in 2020, rising to 5.2% of GDP cumulatively over 2020–21. [Strength: high; Sources: S005]
- In three studied mature federations (US, Canada, Australia), fiscal transfers offset roughly 4–11% of idiosyncratic regional shocks and 13–24% of permanent shocks, operating mainly through automatic stabilizers embedded in a central budget rather than discretionary center-to-subnational transfers. [Strength: high; Sources: S004]
- EU legal primacy is a principle developed through CJEU jurisprudence, not anchored in an explicit primary treaty text; national constitutional courts have in a number of cases refused to draw the consequences of CJEU judgments. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- The vast majority of national courts still apply the EU primacy principle despite documented challenges. [Strength: high; Sources: S006]
- Germany's Basic Law Article 31 states federal law takes precedence over Land law, anchoring supremacy in primary constitutional text rather than jurisprudential doctrine alone. [Strength: high; Sources: S007]
- Monetary accommodation by a union-wide central bank can sustain a currency union for a while but not permanently when sufficiently large asymmetric shocks occur; fiscal transfers are modeled as the more effective long-run retention tool. [Strength: high; Sources: S008]
- The Latin Monetary Union, Scandinavian Monetary Union, and Austro-Hungarian Monetary Union all failed to withstand the shock of World War I, attributed in part to missing common institutions including a common central bank and unified fiscal policy. [Strength: high; Sources: S009, S017]
- Brexit is identified in the literature as the clearest sign of European disintegration to date; the Schengen crisis displayed considerable disintegrative features; disintegration largely did not unfold during the Eurozone crisis. [Strength: high; Sources: S013]
- A standard definition of European disintegration is still lacking in the scholarly literature. [Strength: high; Sources: S013]
- Differentiated disintegration, as defined by Schimmelfennig, is the selective reduction of a state's level and scope of integration; it is analytically distinct from full exit and full collapse of formal intactness. [Strength: high; Sources: S014]
- Euro-area sovereign spread differentials during the crisis were shaped by a dynamic, common time-varying factor and increasingly by country-specific solvency concerns from October 2008 onward. [Strength: high; Sources: S016]
- Redenomination and fragmentation risk in the euro area are state-dependent and responsive to ECB policy announcements, not a stable background constant. [Strength: high; Sources: S018]
- The ECB's Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) can be activated against unwarranted, disorderly market dynamics threatening monetary-policy transmission across the euro area. [Strength: high; Sources: S020]
- Differentiated integration and disintegration in the EU—including opt-outs and outer-core arrangements—can coexist with continuing formal Union membership and a still-functioning legal order. [Strength: high; Sources: S019]

# 3. IDENTIFIED CONTRADICTIONS
- Whether discretionary crisis mechanisms provide durable or only temporary survival benefit
  - Claim A: Euro-area automatic stabilizers and ECB policy tools provide meaningful cushioning (10–30% of GDP shock) and second-generation automatic stabilizers can deliver larger, more rules-based responses than ordinary discretionary action; this supports a meaningful discretionary_backstop_reduction term. [Sources: S005]
  - Claim B: Central-bank monetary accommodation can sustain a currency union only for a while, not permanently; a sequence of large asymmetric shocks can still eventually break the union if fiscal transfer capacity is absent; therefore discretionary_backstop_reduction should be smaller, scenario-contingent, or explicitly temporary. [Sources: S008]
  - Why it matters: The magnitude and durability of the discretionary_backstop_reduction term is a primary driver of the failure probability estimate through 2035. If S005's cushioning is treated as durable, the probability is materially lower; if S008's temporariness constraint binds, the discount must shrink or be conditioned on fiscal-transfer capacity that the EU currently lacks.
- Whether historical monetary union failure cases are usable as a direct base-rate anchor
  - Claim A: Historical monetary unions (Latin, Scandinavian, Austro-Hungarian) are usable as a reference class that keeps failure risk above zero and informs the prior, with lessons explicitly framed for EMU. [Sources: S009]
  - Claim B: These same historical cases are dominated by severe external-shock conditions (WWI) and are not cleanly transferable as unconditional hazard rates; the modern EMU has a materially different resilience architecture. Using them as a base rate and then applying a separate stress multiplier risks double-counting the same shock channel. [Sources: S017, S020]
  - Why it matters: If the historical reference class already embeds severe-shock conditioning, importing it as base_failure_probability and then multiplying by a stress_escalation_multiplier inflates the estimate. The model structure (base × multiplier independence) is not supported unless the base is drawn from a calmer reference class or the terms are explicitly conditioned.
- Durability of discretionary vs. standing fiscal stabilization in supporting union survival
  - Claim A: Durable survival in mature federations is supported by embedded, standing automatic stabilizers in a central budget, not primarily by discretionary center-to-subnational transfers. [Sources: S004]
  - Claim B: The euro area does possess meaningful automatic stabilizers (0.48–0.56 semi-elasticity) and second-generation instruments that can deliver larger crisis responses than ordinary discretion. [Sources: S005]
  - Why it matters: S004 establishes that the EU's reliance on discretionary crisis response rather than a standing central fiscal mechanism is a genuine fragility relative to mature federations. S005 shows non-trivial existing stabilizers. The tension is whether the EU's existing automatic stabilizers are sufficient to close the gap or whether the absence of a standing central budget transfers risks that cannot be discretionarily offset. Neither source resolves this quantitatively for the 2035 horizon.

# 4. EPISTEMIC VOIDS (OPEN PROBLEMS / UNKNOWNS)
- Formal, operationally precise definition of material_union_failure that is defensible against the multiple-modes-of-disintegration critique.
  - Why it matters: Without a precise event boundary, P(material_union_failure by 2035-01-01) is not a well-formed probability object. Intermediate states—differentiated disintegration, selective opt-outs, outer-core arrangements, Schengen-style rollbacks—may or may not satisfy the target event depending on the definition chosen.
  - Blocking effect: All numerical outputs are uninterpretable until the event boundary is specified. Sources S012, S013, S014, S015, S019 collectively establish this is unresolved.
- An unconditional base hazard rate for material_union_failure drawn from a reference class that is not already conditioned on severe external shocks.
  - Why it matters: The available historical reference class (S009, S017) is dominated by WWI-era cases. Using it as base_failure_probability while also applying a stress_escalation_multiplier double-counts the stress channel. A calmer-period reference class or an explicitly conditional model structure is needed.
  - Blocking effect: base_failure_probability cannot be set without resolving whether the reference-class conditioning is compatible with the intended model structure.
- The hazard function shape over the 2026–2035 horizon: whether failure hazard is constant, front-loaded around crisis episodes, or reduced by post-2012 institutional reforms and post-pandemic policy responses.
  - Why it matters: Horizon extrapolation from a 2007 ten-year assessment to 2035 is not defensible without an explicit hazard-rate assumption. Fragmentation and redenomination risk are documented as state-dependent and policy-sensitive (S018), not a stable background constant.
  - Blocking effect: Any point probability for 2035 implicitly assumes a hazard function. Without making that function explicit, the estimate cannot be validated or updated as conditions change.
- Quantitative bounds for stress_escalation_multiplier grounded in historically observed euro-area stress regimes.
  - Why it matters: S003 flags this as the dominant variance driver in the model. S016 shows sovereign stress is episode-bound and dynamic, not a scalar, but does not yield the multiplier itself. The [1.30, 1.70] candidate range cited in S020 is explicitly not validated.
  - Blocking effect: The multiplier is currently the main free parameter in the model. Without bounded calibration, any probability output is dominated by this unconstrained term.
- Structured expert elicitation for all model multipliers (stress_escalation_multiplier, legal_break_multiplier, discretionary_backstop_reduction, base_failure_probability).
  - Why it matters: S010 establishes that mapping qualitative fragility evidence to numerical probability objects requires a disciplined elicitation protocol with outer-range-first sequencing, quantified verbal terms, and explicit sensitivity checks. No such protocol has been conducted.
  - Blocking effect: Without elicitation or a clearly bounded sensitivity protocol, all multiplier values are freehand judgments that cannot be defended against a validator.
- Whether the EU's existing automatic stabilizers (documented in S005) are sufficient to substitute for the standing central-budget mechanism that mature federations rely on (S004), or whether a material fiscal-transfer gap remains open through 2035.
  - Why it matters: The discretionary_backstop_reduction term depends on resolving this. If the gap is large and unclosed, the reduction is bounded downward by S008's temporariness constraint. If the EU's instruments are closer to a standing mechanism than S004's comparator implies, the reduction could be larger.
  - Blocking effect: Sizing the survival-mechanism discount requires a quantitative comparison between the EU's stabilizer architecture and the mature-federation benchmark, which no source in this collection provides directly.
- Whether any of the documented disintegrative features (primacy challenges, Schengen rollbacks, selective opt-outs) would individually or cumulatively satisfy the material_union_failure event boundary if that boundary were formally specified.
  - Why it matters: The legal_break_multiplier depends on knowing whether observable legal-contestation events (S006) cross the event boundary or remain on the formal_intactness side per the ontology established in S014 and S019.
  - Blocking effect: legal_break_multiplier calibration is blocked until event ontology is resolved.

# 6. CANDIDATE CLAIMS TO TEST
1. The EU's existing automatic fiscal stabilizers (semi-elasticity 0.48–0.56; absorbing 10–30% of a standard GDP shock) are insufficient to substitute for the standing central-budget mechanism that mature federations rely on for permanent shock absorption.
   Why testable: S004 provides a quantitative benchmark for mature-federation stabilizer performance. S005 provides EU stabilizer magnitudes. The gap between EU stabilizer output and mature-federation benchmarks can be computed. Testable by comparing the EU's 2.8% / 5.2% GDP COVID contribution against the 13–24% permanent shock absorption found in US/Canada/Australia.
   Depends on: mature_federation_permanent_shock_absorption, euro_area_automatic_stabilizer_gdp_shock_cushion, formal definition of what counts as a standing vs. discretionary mechanism
   Priority: high | Sources: S004, S005
2. The historical monetary union reference class (Latin, Scandinavian, Austro-Hungarian) cannot be used as an unconditional base_failure_probability while a separate stress_escalation_multiplier is also present in the model, because both terms partially capture the same severe-shock channel.
   Why testable: Testable by examining whether the historical failure cases are conditioned on extreme shocks (WWI) and whether the proposed stress_escalation_multiplier also captures extreme shocks; if both are true, the model structure produces double-counting. Resolution requires either a calmer reference class or explicit conditioning.
   Depends on: base_failure_probability specification, stress_escalation_multiplier definition, whether historical union failures are treated as unconditional draws or conditional on severe shock
   Priority: high | Sources: S009, S017, S020
3. Discretionary and ECB-led crisis management reduces near-term EU failure probability materially but does not provide a durable reduction through the full 2035 horizon absent a fiscal-transfer capacity increase.
   Why testable: S008 provides the theoretical mechanism (accommodation is temporary). S005 provides the magnitude of existing stabilizers. Testable by examining whether any post-2012 institutional reforms have materially closed the fiscal-transfer capacity gap identified in S004 and S008. Can be partially falsified if standing fiscal capacity has increased enough to change the durability assessment.
   Depends on: discretionary_backstop_reduction magnitude, whether post-2012 reforms (ESM, NGEU, TPI) constitute a standing or discretionary mechanism, fiscal transfer capacity relative to S004 benchmark
   Priority: high | Sources: S005, S008, S004, S020
4. The EU primacy contestation channel (documented in S006) does not by itself justify a large legal_break_multiplier because the vast majority of national courts still apply primacy and documented refusals have not yet produced a durable rupture of the legal order.
   Why testable: S006 provides both the evidence of contestation and the countervailing evidence that most courts comply. Testable by examining whether any observed primacy refusal has escalated to a systemic legal rupture or durable non-compliance. Can be falsified if new cases cross from episodic refusal to institutionalized non-application.
   Depends on: material_union_failure event boundary definition, legal_break_multiplier calibration, whether episodic non-compliance maps to failure event
   Priority: medium | Sources: S006, S007, S019
5.    Why testable: S012, S013, S014, S019 collectively document multiple disintegration modes that fall short of total collapse. Testable by checking whether any proposed event definition explicitly maps each documented mode to either material_union_failure or formal_intactness.    Priority: high | Sources: S012, S013, S014, S015, S019

   Why testable: S018 directly documents the state-dependency and policy-sensitivity of redenomination risk. Testable by examining whether the proposed model specifies a hazard function (constant, front-loaded, post-reform-reduced) or implicitly assumes constancy. Falsifiable if the model explicitly specifies a non-constant hazard function with a defended shape.
   Depends on: hazard function shape over 2026–2035, whether post-2012 institutional reforms are modeled as reducing hazard rate, TPI and post-pandemic instruments as time-varying backstops
