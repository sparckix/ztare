## Claim

If the observed `z(x₁, x₂)` represents a generative process governed
by competitive algebraic limits — an initial activation capacity
proportional to `x₁` plus a cooperative structural-saturation limit
proportional to `(x₁ + p₁)^{−p₂}` — and `x₂` is a strictly linear
resource scalar, then the output is a *structurally invariant
algebraic rational decay*. This cooperative algebraic saturation
*locally mimics* an exponential barrier over finite domains, but
fundamentally asymptotes to a strict power law. The named
discriminator is the **asymptotic stabilization of the apparent
power-law elasticity** `α = −d ln z / d ln x₁`: the algebraic thesis
guarantees a strict mathematical upper bound `α ≤ p₂ − 1`; a true
exponential rival predicts `α` diverges linearly to infinity. The
judge independently named the exponential-exclusion gap *without
ground-truth access*. Apparatus-internal champion score: **94 / 100**.

## What this calibrates

Together with `gp080_01`, this is the canonical INS-018 instance of
the *underdetermination boundary*: two structurally distinct theses
each pass the holdout gate with high scores (98 and 94), and only the
farther-tail and the elasticity discriminator distinguish them. The
takeaway recorded in the public claim register: a clean holdout pass
is necessary but *not sufficient* — the apparatus needs the
farther-tail and named structural discriminators alongside.
