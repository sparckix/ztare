# GP-160 Asymptotic Wall — Public Claim Summary

> Public-evidence surface for the grammar-ceiling diagnosis. Working
> directory private; cited by `docs/public_claim_register.md` under
> *Apparatus Self-Audits*.

## Claim

Additional compute does not break a structural ceiling imposed by the
expression grammar — *only grammar expansion* (adding a new primitive
or composition layer) enables structural-class transitions. The
project demonstrates the asymmetry on a discriminator suite: 63
iterations under a restricted grammar fail to reach a form that one
grammar extension produces in 6 iterations. The wall is structural,
not search-budget. Apparatus-internal champion score: **82 / 100**.

## What this catalogues

INS-020 and INS-021 — the **compute-insensitive ceiling**. The
diagnosis distinguishes between (a) grammar ceilings (can the answer
be written in the language?) and (b) search-budget ceilings (does the
mutator find it under more iterations?). The two are routinely
conflated; the project sealing this distinction with a controlled
experiment is the durable artifact.

The companion finding in the experimental-math cluster — the sopfr
A001414 grammar-vs-space distinction (grammar admits the answer; the
mutator does not enter the right mathematical category) — is the
*space* ceiling. Together they bound the apparatus's structural
constraints on either side.

## Retest tag

*Diagnostic finding.* The ceiling is structural; the artifact is the
demonstration that compute does not break it.
