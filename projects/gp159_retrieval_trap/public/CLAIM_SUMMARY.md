# GP-159 Retrieval Trap — Public Claim Summary

> Public-evidence surface for the retrieval-trap failure-mode
> discovery. Working directory private; cited by
> `docs/public_claim_register.md` under *Apparatus Self-Audits*.

## Claim

When visible data is generated by `y = a/(x + b)` with non-standard
constants, the apparatus's three-parameter generalization
`y = a/(x+b)^c` returns `c = 1.0000` deterministically (not merely
`≈ 1`), because the third parameter has no residual improvement to
allocate. The structural-uniqueness claim of the trap — that all
three-parameter monotonic rivals with `1/x` tails and `K_law ≤ 3`
either collapse to the two-parameter form under optimization or
produce higher RMSE on visible data — was strengthened from "weakest
node" status (`p = 0.55`) to a sealed structural finding by
exhaustive enumeration of the rival space under the allowed grammar.
Apparatus-internal champion score: **90 / 100**.

## What this catalogues

The result is a *named failure mode* — a "retrieval trap" — where the
apparatus's grammar admits a three-parameter form but the data has
no third dimension to identify, so any rival also collapses, and the
selection rule does not detect the over-parametrization. The
structural-uniqueness finding (no `K_law ≤ 3` three-parameter monotone
with `1/x` tail produces a lower max-residual than `0.01070` on the 11
visible points) is the discriminator that protects the apparatus from
falsely promoting the over-parametrized form. Recorded as INS-006
adjacent.

## Retest tag

*Diagnostic finding.* The trap is a structural property of the
grammar + data combination, not a single-run artifact; it cannot be
"retested" in the recovery sense — the diagnosis *is* the result.
