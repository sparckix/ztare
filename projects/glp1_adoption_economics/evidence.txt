## Novo Nordisk GLP-1 U.S. Price Cuts: Factual Summary

### Pre-Cut Baseline Prices


Wegovy's list price was approximately $1,350/month, while Ozempic and Rybelsus ran at approximately $1,000/month.

 

Health economists at ICER estimated that current net prices of about $7,000–$9,000 per year (roughly $600–$750/month) already sat within the usual U.S. cost-effectiveness range for obesity treatment — roughly half to two-thirds of original list prices.



---

### February 2026: Major WAC List Price Cuts (Effective Jan. 1, 2027)


Novo announced plans to cut the U.S. wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) of its semaglutide products to **$675/month**, effective January 1, 2027, applying to the full suite including both injectable and oral Wegovy, injectable Ozempic, and oral Rybelsus.

 

For Wegovy, this represents a **50% reduction** from its current list price; for Ozempic, it is a **35% reduction**.





Notably, the new list prices do not affect direct-to-patient, self-pay prescriptions. Citi analyst Geoffrey Meacham noted the move will likely benefit only a small portion of new prescriptions, since cash-pay channels now hold an "overwhelming share."



---

### November 2025: Trump/TrumpRx Deal & Medicare/Medicaid Pricing


President Trump announced deals with Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk to lower GLP-1 drug prices on Medicare and Medicaid, and to offer treatments directly to consumers at a discount on a new platform called **TrumpRx.gov**.



Key price points from the deal:
- 

Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly agreed to cut the price Medicare pays for GLP-1s to **$245/month**, extending that government price across all 50 Medicaid programs for all covered uses.


- 

Certain Medicare patients will pay a copay of **$50/month** for approved uses of injectable and oral GLP-1 drugs.


- 

Starting doses of upcoming obesity pills will be **$149/month** for everyone getting them through Medicare, Medicaid, or TrumpRx.


- 

Consumers buying injectable GLP-1 medications directly from the companies will pay an average of **$350/month** to start, with drugmakers committed to reducing that price to about **$250** over the next two years.

### November 2025: Self-Pay Channel Price Cuts


Novo offered the first two monthly doses of Wegovy and Ozempic for **$199** (expiring end of March), and reduced the self-pay price for other patients to **$349/month**, down from $499/month.

 

For comparison, compounding pharmacy Hims & Hers charges as little as $199/month for compounded semaglutide.

## Medicaid GLP-1 Obesity Coverage by State — January 2026

### Overall Coverage Landscape



As of January 2026, only **13 state Medicaid programs** cover GLP-1s for obesity treatment under fee-for-service (FFS).

 This represents a notable contraction from recent months: 

KFF's 2025 Medicaid budget survey found **16 state Medicaid programs** covered GLP-1s for obesity as of October 2025; however, four states — **California, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, and South Carolina** — have since eliminated coverage, likely reflecting state budget challenges and significant drug costs.

 

North Carolina also eliminated GLP-1 coverage beginning October 2025 due to a budget stalemate, but coverage was reinstated in December 2025, contributing to the net count of 13 as of January 2026.



### Legal and Structural Framework



States may decide independently whether to cover obesity drugs under Medicaid. While the Medicaid Drug Rebate Program generally requires coverage of nearly all FDA-approved drugs, weight-loss drugs are included in a small group of drugs that can be statutorily excluded from coverage.

 

As a result, GLP-1 coverage for obesity is optional for states, while coverage is required for drugs approved for type 2 diabetes and, since March 2024 and December 2024, for cardiovascular disease (Wegovy) and moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea.



### Key State-Level Actions (Effective January 1, 2026)

- **California (Medi-Cal):** 

Effective January 1, 2026, Medi-Cal Rx no longer covers Saxenda, Wegovy, or Zepbound when used for weight loss.



- **Pennsylvania:** 

Pennsylvania Medicaid stopped covering GLP-1s for weight loss for adults 21 and older starting January 1, 2026.

 

Children and young adults under 21 may still receive GLP-1s for obesity, as federal law requires Medicaid to cover all medically necessary treatments for those under 21.



- **Michigan:** 

Effective January 1, 2026, Michigan's Medicaid program began limiting coverage of GLP-1s for obesity treatment and long-term weight management — a significant change for a state that was among the first to cover GLP-1s for Medicaid beneficiaries.

 

GLP-1 medications are now covered only for beneficiaries with a BMI ≥ 40 (morbid obesity) who meet strict prior authorization criteria.

 

This policy is projected to save the Michigan Medicaid program **$240 million in 2026**.

 

Up to one million Michiganders who are overweight or obese could lose coverage as a result.



### Utilization Controls and Trend



When GLP-1s are covered, they are typically subject to utilization controls such as prior authorization, which can further limit access.

 

The number of Medicaid prescriptions and gross spending on GLP-1s have both increased substantially since 2019.

 

A few other states are planning or considering obesity drug restrictions in state fiscal year 2026 or 2027, and state interest in expanding coverage is waning, with cost cited as the key factor in coverage decisions.
