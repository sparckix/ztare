# Displacement-transitive residual: pencil analysis

Frozen before the displacement-regime database audit on 2026-07-16.

## Eigenquestion

Does the common-prime row-cycle pattern survive in the exact complement of
Castelli's finite-primitive-level argument: finite indecomposable cycle sets for
which `Dis(X)` acts transitively?

## Published boundary

For a finite indecomposable cycle set, Castelli's Theorem 22 identifies finite
primitive level with intransitivity of `Dis(X)`. Proposition 29 then passes to a
prime-size trivial quotient and proves that one prime divisor of `|X|` divides
every cycle length in every left multiplication.

The unrestricted residual therefore has the following inputs:

- `G(X)` acts transitively;
- `Dis(X)` acts transitively;
- the cycle-set and nondegeneracy laws hold;
- no prime-size trivial quotient is available through the Proposition 29 route.

## Candidate statements

Strong candidate:

> For every finite indecomposable cycle set `X`, there is a prime `p | |X|`
> dividing every nontrivial cycle length in every left multiplication.

Question-30 candidate:

> No left multiplication of a finite indecomposable cycle set has a nontrivial
> cycle of length coprime to `|X|`.

The strong candidate implies the second. The finite database can kill either;
it cannot establish either statement without an unrestricted argument.

## Group/brace coordinate

In the standard brace-coset presentation `X = B/K`, each left multiplication
is the action on `B/K` of an element from the transitive cycle base. A cycle
through `yK` has length

`[<b> : <b> ∩ yKy⁻¹]`.

The missing bridge is a reason, forced by the brace and cycle-base structure,
that these indices share a prime divisor of `[B:K]`. Ordinary transitive group
actions do not have this property, so a proof must use cycle-set structure.

## Discriminating finite test

For every indecomposable table in the pinned orders 8–10 database:

1. recompute the orbit of the group generated by
   `σ_x σ_0⁻¹`, which generates `Dis(X)`;
2. split models into displacement-intransitive and displacement-transitive;
3. compare row-cycle spectra and common-prime supports in both strata;
4. recover Castelli's published finite-primitive-level counts at orders 8 and
   9 as a matched checksum.

## Kill conditions

- Any displacement-transitive model with a nontrivial coprime row cycle kills
  both candidates and supplies a bounded counterexample to Question 30.
- Any model with no common prime support kills the strong candidate while
  leaving Question 30 open.
- A mismatch with the published order-8/9 finite-primitive-level counts kills
  the displacement implementation.
- Persistence only on prime-power carrier sizes is recorded as finite evidence;
  it earns no theorem claim.

## Intended formal surface

Lean formalization waits for either a checked counterexample or a named
brace/group lemma that controls the coset-cycle index above. Encoding the
database observation itself would add no unrestricted authority.

## Second loop: singularity fork (frozen 2026-07-16 before group-order audit)

Castelli notes after Question 30 that a counterexample must be a singular cycle
set: some prime divides the order of the permutation left brace `G(X)` but not
`|X|`. The same paper asks whether singular cycle sets without finite primitive
level exist. The 33 displacement-transitive database examples are the exact
small-order locus for that question.

### Eigenquestion

Does the displacement-transitive complement contain a singular cycle set?

### Competing explanations

1. **Known-obstruction explanation.** Every hard-regime example is
   non-singular. Its lack of a coprime row cycle is then compatible with the
   already-published necessary condition and supplies little new mechanism.
2. **Singular hard-regime witness.** At least one hard-regime example has a
   prime divisor of `|G(X)|` absent from `|X|`. Since `Dis(X)` is transitive, it
   has no finite primitive level by Theorem 22. A checked table would therefore
   answer Castelli's singular-without-finite-primitive-level challenge at finite
   order, even if it still does not answer Question 30.

### Discriminating test

For all 33 pinned displacement-transitive examples, enumerate the exact groups
generated by the left translations and by `σ_x σ_0⁻¹`; record their orders and
prime supports. Independently replay transitivity and the cycle-set laws. Any
singular witness is then rechecked by a second group engine or a separately
implemented closure before promotion.

### Kill conditions

- A group-order implementation that disagrees across independent closures is
  rejected.
- A prime outside `|X|` without transitive `Dis(X)` does not answer the hard
  challenge.
- Absence through order 10 is bounded evidence only and earns no theorem or
  novelty claim.

### Intended formal surface

A positive result requires an explicit finite table plus kernel-checkable
cycle-set, indecomposability, displacement-transitivity, and permutation-group
order certificates. A negative database result needs no Lean encoding.

## Third loop: published singular-construction cycle fork

Frozen 2026-07-16 before enumerating any cycle of the Cedó--Okniński
construction in Theorem 4.2 of arXiv:2407.07907.

### Eigenquestion

Does the first admissible published singular simple solution already contain a
left-translation cycle whose nontrivial length is coprime to the carrier size?

Theorem 4.2 constructs a simple solution on
`X = Z/(p^n) × Z/(p^n)` with `|X| = p^(2n)` and a prime `q | p - 1` dividing
the order of `G(X)`. Its proof shows specifically that `q` divides the order of
`σ_(0,0)`. This does not alone imply that an individual cycle of `σ_(0,0)` has
length coprime to `|X|`: the witnessed cycle lengths could all be divisible by
both `p` and `q`.

### Competing outcomes

1. **Coprime cycle.** Some cycle of a published left translation has length
   `q` (or another nontrivial `p`-coprime length). Since Theorem 4.2 makes the
   solution simple and hence indecomposable, the construction supplies a
   negative answer to Castelli's Question 30, whether or not the paper states
   that corollary.
2. **Mixed cycles only.** Every nontrivial cycle carrying the singular prime
   also has a `p` factor. The published singular construction answers the
   no-finite-primitive-level question but leaves Question 30 unresolved.

### Discriminating test

Instantiate the least admissible parameters `p = 7`, `n = 1`, `q = 3`, with
`t(c) = 2c mod 7`. Construct the paper's `j` family orbit-by-orbit, enumerate
the exact permutations `σ_(a,b)`, and record their complete cycle profiles.
Independently check bijectivity, the finite cycle-set/YBE identity in the
left-translation convention, transitivity, and the formula for `j` before
using any cycle as evidence.

### Kill conditions

- Failure of any construction checksum rejects the implementation.
- A cycle of length divisible by `p` does not answer Question 30 merely because
  its length is also divisible by `q`.
- If a coprime cycle exists, novelty remains unconfirmed until an exact
  literature search checks whether the corollary was already stated or cited.

### Intended formal surface

A positive finite witness should be exported as a small deterministic
certificate first. Lean encoding is warranted only if the published theorem
does not already state the Question-30 consequence and the translation from
its construction to the cycle-set convention has been checked exactly.
