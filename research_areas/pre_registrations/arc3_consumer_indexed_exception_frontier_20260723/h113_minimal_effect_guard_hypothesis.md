# H113: chronological minimal effect guard

Date: 2026-08-06

Hypothesis:
`H-GPSA-CHRONOLOGICAL-MINIMAL-EFFECT-GUARD-20260806-113`

Status: pre-registered after the H112 result and the aggregate effect-family
retrospective, before chronological guard fitting or holdout scoring

## Trigger

The guarded compiler retains exact endpoint variants, while the existing
effect-option layer correctly quotients endpoints into 21 relative-effect
families. Fifteen of those families contain multiple exact initiation states,
but that full-corpus fact does not show that the family can be selected before
executing an option in a later state. Biological motor gating predicts a
sensory consequence from a compressed present-state signature and abstains
when the signature is outside its learned domain.

## Eigenquestion

Can the smallest unambiguous projection of the existing factor/history key
predict a retained motor word's relative effect on chronologically later,
exact-state-unseen windows?

## Hypothesis

For at least one operation word learned from the first 21 frozen H63
trajectories, a proper subset of the named initiation coordinates predicts its
relative effect without discovery collisions and correctly covers at least
five exact-state-unseen windows in at least two of the final seven
trajectories. Operation word alone must be ambiguous for that word.

## Discriminating test

Using only the frozen H63 graph and chronology:

1. verify the H63 action-system, carrier-execution, and projection identities;
2. compile guarded programs from trajectories 1--21 only;
3. enumerate every ordinary occurrence of those frozen operation words in the
   discovery and holdout traces;
4. expose the initiation key through its named coordinates:
   `controlled_base`, `finite_configuration`,
   `operation_domain_assignment`,
   `ordered_feasibility_configuration`, `ordered_budget`,
   `one_shot_availability`, history suffix, and predictive context when
   present;
5. for each program enumerate coordinate subsets in size-then-lexicographic
   order and retain the first subset whose discovery guard keys each map to
   exactly one ordered relative-effect signature;
6. freeze the resulting guard map, then score the final seven trajectories;
7. separate exact-state repeats from windows whose full initiation identity
   was absent in discovery; abstain when a learned guard key is absent;
8. compare against operation-only and full-exact-state gates; mutate split,
   coordinate subset, program word, predicted effect, and source identity in
   receipt-only controls.

No environment or model/controller call is permitted. Holdout effects cannot
participate in subset selection or guard fitting.

## Success criterion

Stage A is supported only if one fixed program satisfies all of:

- discovery contains at least two effect signatures for the operation word,
  so operation-only prediction is ambiguous;
- the selected guard is a proper subset of the full initiation coordinates
  and is collision-free on discovery;
- at least five exact-state-unseen holdout windows across at least two trace
  references have guard keys witnessed in discovery;
- every covered unseen window matches the frozen predicted effect signature;
- at least two predicted effect signatures are exercised across all covered
  holdout windows;
- unseen guard keys abstain; and
- all identity mutations are detected.

## Kill and refinement rule

Reject if chronological holdout contains no exact-state novelty, every safe
gate is exact-state-sized, a coarser gate makes one wrong effect prediction,
coverage misses the declared threshold, or the result depends on holdout
fitting. Rejection directs acquisition toward active interventions that vary
factor coordinates. A pass licenses prospective use of the learned gate as an
effect predictor, not task-skill promotion.

## Claim boundary

Passing would establish chronological generalization of one relative-effect
gate within one frozen game graph. It would not establish task credit,
task-boundary transfer, controller benefit, benchmark gain, catalytic
learning, criticality, takeoff, or literature novelty.
