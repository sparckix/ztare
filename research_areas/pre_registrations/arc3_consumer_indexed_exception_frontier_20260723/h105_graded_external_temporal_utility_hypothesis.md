# H105 graded external temporal utility

Date: 2026-08-07

Hypothesis:
`H-GPSA-GRADED-EXTERNAL-TEMPORAL-UTILITY-20260807-105`

Status: pre-registered; retrospective analysis of prospectively collected H95
evidence plus anonymous negative controls

## Eigenquestion

Does binary `attained/open` settlement discard externally adjudicated
efficiency value that is necessary for internal judgment, and can a separate
graded utility channel recover that value without importing information gain,
changing primitive costs, or crossing exact decision authority?

## Hypothesis

A matched temporal pair may carry a graded external-utility contrast when both
arms share:

- the exact task contract, decision namespace, source choice context,
  continuation controller, and complete option set;
- one frozen external utility-measure identity and component weights;
- the same primitive action cost; and
- distinct arm, option-family, option-variant, and evidence identities.

The pair's externally measured value may distinguish two arms whose terminal
task statuses are both `attained`. Such a contrast is utility evidence only.
It cannot manufacture terminal task discharge, information-yield calibration,
or cross-context transport.

H95 is a useful pre-existing discriminator. Its two randomized pairs share one
exact target choice surface and equal action budgets. Pair 1 has a terminal
task contrast and composite delta `+0.89`; pair 2 has equal terminal task
status and composite delta `+0.07`, because the causal arm completed earlier.
The existing binary temporal-credit compiler should therefore remain
undersampled at minimum support two, while a graded compiler should retain
both positive external-value contrasts.

## Discriminating test

1. Compile an exact external utility measure from H95's pre-registered
   `task_score_weight=0.8` and `efficiency_score_weight=0.2`.
2. Lower the two saved H95 randomized pairs into four utility-arm receipts
   using their frozen scope, intervention revisions, primitive costs,
   component values, terminal task results, and external evidence refs.
3. Settle each pair and aggregate exact-authority option judgments with
   minimum support two.
4. Compare the result with the binary `attained/open` baseline.
5. Change the task, source context, continuation controller, complete option
   set, utility measure, primitive cost, pair identity, option identity, or
   evidence identity.
6. Add a high-information pair whose external utility values are equal.
7. Attempt to pool H96's failed lineage-transport arm into the H95 authority.

## Success criterion

1. H95 pair 1 settles with external delta `+0.89`.
2. H95 pair 2 settles with external delta `+0.07` despite equal terminal task
   statuses.
3. The causal option receives graded preference support two; the placebo
   option receives graded hazard support two.
4. Mean settled delta is `+0.48`, matching the frozen H95 result.
5. The binary baseline remains below support two.
6. All authority, measure, pair, option, evidence, and equal-cost mismatches
   refuse settlement.
7. Equal external utility remains uninformative regardless of information
   yield.
8. Primitive costs are byte-for-byte unchanged by compilation.
9. H96 cannot be pooled without a separate transport certificate.
10. Receipts explicitly deny terminal task-credit and information-yield
    authority.

## Kill conditions

- terminal status is inferred from a utility scalar;
- component weights are changed after reading an arm outcome;
- information gain changes utility status;
- unequal primitive costs are silently compared;
- utility crosses any exact authority component;
- H96 is pooled by label or semantic similarity;
- a tie becomes positive support; or
- aggregation changes an arm's recorded cost or external value.

## Claim boundary

Passing establishes a graded, exact-authority analysis of previously collected
H95 external outcomes. It does not establish a new ARC score, prospective
H105 environment contact, multi-decision chain collection, cross-context
transport, H97 support, or benchmark improvement.
