# H103: endogenous residual reaction from a settled failure

Date: 2026-08-06

Status: pre-registered; compiler and live discriminator not yet run

## Correction to H98-H102

H98-H102 establish a synthetic path from supplied residual candidates to
rank-quotiented children, sparse settlement, multigeneration lineage, a
food-generated catalytic set, and a state-dependent kinetic threshold. They
do not show that a controller can manufacture the residual questions that
feed this path. Human-authored candidates make the reaction network
exogenous; under that condition, apparent compounding can be continued by the
experimenter even if the controller has no self-directed inquiry mechanism.

## Brain-inspired conjecture

The relevant biological analogy is a separation of roles: episodic indexing
preserves which prior event is being replayed; offline replay perturbs that
event into counterfactual response directions; executive control chooses a
small assay; external consequences update which directions survive. The
analogy does not establish the algorithm. H103 compiles its falsifiable core:
one exact settled failure must causally produce several pre-outcome,
independent questions whose proposed measurements can later be checked.

## Eigenquestion

Can one exact settled failure and stored reasoning parent cause a controller
to propose at least three rank-independent residual response directions and
freeze a four-trajectory sparse causal assay before any descendant outcome is
observed?

## Hypothesis

Bind the proposal to an exact authority containing:

- task, controller class, context, choice set, and action vocabulary;
- controller instance and stored parent response identities;
- source response family, source program, causal derivative, settled-failure,
  intervention-revision, and source-history-prefix identities;
- the ordered measurement-axis catalog and primitive intervention-cost unit;
- exact parent-child lineage, generation index, prompt bytes, raw controller
  output, and the complete allowed input-evidence set.

The controller output is parsed into local question handles, content hashes,
exact rational response signatures over the declared axes, predicted
information yield, offline replay cost, and interactions declared before
outcome. Question semantics remain in the caller; the common kernel receives
only identity, exact signatures, cost, and evidence.

The compiler must reuse H98 `compile_residual_fission` to quotient
positive-scalar copies and select an exact independent basis. It must reuse
H100 `compile_sparse_settlement_schedule` to freeze the smallest collision-free
Walsh design for the declared main effects and interactions. It must not
contain ARC-specific state or solution rules.

## Stage A: offline mechanism discriminator

Use a fixture representing a frozen controller output with four proposed
questions: three independent response directions and one positive-scalar copy.
Require:

1. four raw questions quotient to three direction classes and exact rank
   three;
2. the scalar copy cannot increase offspring capacity;
3. the selected basis inherits one authority and one exact parent lineage;
4. the pre-outcome additive schedule has four trajectories, model rank four
   including the intercept, versus eight full-factorial trajectories;
5. the receipt says `live_settlement_required=true`,
   `compounding_supported=false`, and `takeoff_supported=false`.

The fixture is authored test data. A Stage-A pass supports only the compiler,
not autonomous question generation.

## Stage B: live causal discriminator

Stage B is gated on a settled H97 descendant child. From one exact stored
blind controller parent, fork:

- causal: supply the authority-bound failure derivative and no descendant
  outcome;
- placebo: supply a byte-matched true but irrelevant context object with no
  access to the causal derivative or its question identities.

Freeze both structured proposals before descendant environment contact.
Compile each through the unchanged Stage-A kernel. Execute the causal sparse
schedule only after the proposal is frozen, then settle predicted versus
observed information yield with fresh observations. No controller-generated
rank or information-yield prediction counts as knowledge before that external
settlement.

## Stage-B success criterion

The causal proposal compiles at least two independent children; at least two
receive positive externally settled effects; false-edge reproduction remains
below one; predicted information yield passes the frozen calibration rule;
the causal schedule costs less than its factorial counterfactual; every child
is content-bound to the H97 parent and can serve as a fresh next-generation
input. The placebo may propose questions, but it may not reuse causal
identities or settlement evidence and must not receive causal promotion from
the causal trials.

## Negative fixtures

- Missing task, context, controller, choice-set, or action-vocabulary identity.
- Controller-instance, stored-parent-response, response-family, derivative,
  settled-failure, intervention-revision, history-prefix, or generation drift.
- Prompt or raw-output content hash drift.
- Question authority different from proposal authority.
- Repeated question handle or response-signature arity different from the
  measurement catalog.
- Zero response direction, duplicate/proportional direction counted twice, or
  a forged rank declaration.
- Question evidence outside the frozen allowed input set.
- Missing settled-failure evidence at the proposal boundary.
- Interaction naming an unselected or unknown question, duplicate
  interaction, or interaction introduced after outcome.
- Proposal constructed after a descendant observation or settlement receipt.
- Cross-lineage child reuse, reused settlement observation, or post-hoc
  replacement of the controller output.
- A synthetic or pre-outcome receipt presented as compounding or takeoff.

## Kill conditions

Kill this route if the controller-input/output binding cannot be checked; if
outcome evidence can enter the proposal unnoticed; if scalar copies increase
rank; if question authority can cross parent, controller, context, choice set,
or generation boundaries; if the assay can change after outcome without a new
identity; if Stage B yields fewer than two supported causal children; if false
edges reproduce at or above replacement; or if predicted information yield
does not calibrate to external observations.

## Compounding criterion

The architecture compounds only if all of the following become measured
rather than assumed:

1. settled failures endogenously generate independent successor questions;
2. those questions causally alter controller behavior and external outcomes;
3. supported children per settled parent exceed one after quotienting copies;
4. children become exact inputs to a fresh generation without evidence reuse;
5. sparse measurement cost grows slower than supported capability output;
6. transport survival and productive reaction rates exceed depreciation while
   false-edge rates remain below replacement.

H95 supports one causal handoff. H97 remains blocked before controller contact.
H98-H102 support only synthetic mechanisms. H103 Stage B is the first test of
the missing endogenous proposal reaction.

## Prior-art and claim boundary

Experience replay, active learning, experimental design, metacognitive
question generation, automated scientific discovery, Gödel-agent-style
self-modification, skill evolution, Walsh designs, and autocatalytic-network
models are established. The candidate contribution is the particular join:
stored-parent controller output bound to an exact settled failure, pre-outcome
residual signatures, quotient/rank authority, sparse causal settlement,
calibrated information yield, false-edge accounting, and content-bound
multigeneration lineage.

Stage A cannot establish autonomous inquiry, live compounding, ARC score gain,
capability takeoff, or literature novelty. Those claims require Stage B and
subsequent multigeneration transport with measured rates.
