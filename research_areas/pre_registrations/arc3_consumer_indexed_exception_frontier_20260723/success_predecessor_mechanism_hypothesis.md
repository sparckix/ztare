# Success-predecessor mechanism transport hypothesis

Date: 2026-07-26

Hypothesis ID: `H-ARC3-SUCCESS-PREDECESSOR-MECHANISM-20260726-26`

## Eigenquestion

Does task consequence transport across level ontologies as an ordered option
mechanism rather than as a property of the terminal source node?

## Hypothesis

After quotienting presentation coordinates and routine control costs, the
maximal continuous trace ending at the epoch-0 completion contains a nontrivial
ordered mechanism-event word. That word embeds in the held-out epoch-1
completion trace more strongly than in every epoch-1 non-discharge terminal
trace. The transport is carried by at least two ordered events with explicit
backward sections to their observed transitions.

## Governing identity

The tested object is a `consequence-anchored option section`: an ordered
sequence of observed mechanism events, its terminal lifecycle edge, and exact
transition lineage. The terminal edge owns lifecycle termination; the external
task receipt supplies positive valence. Neither is stored in an ordinary
control node or mechanism token.

Equality of event tokens ignores rendered coordinates and literal operation
names. A second fixed language may preserve only the equality partition of
operations among event positions.

## Fixed lowering

For every authoritative boundary row, recover only its maximal exact
state/successor-contiguous predecessor section. Coarsen the terminal row to an
unlabeled lifecycle boundary and exclude it from the event word.

For each ordinary transition, compute the accepted factor mechanism and retain
only:

1. controlled-support changes, with only the sign of the support delta;
2. finite-configuration or operation-domain changes;
3. one-shot availability directions;
4. positive ordered-feasibility or ordered-budget renewal.

Pure identity, controlled translation, and nonpositive resource consumption
are routine control costs and do not enter the event word. Collapse adjacent
equal event tokens. Evaluate exactly two preregistered languages:

- `event_family`: the ordered mechanism events;
- `event_operation_partition`: each event plus the restricted-growth code of
  its operation within the event word.

## Discriminating test

Use epoch 0's positive trace as the sole template. Hide every epoch-1 terminal
label, compute longest-common-subsequence F1 against all epoch-1
boundary-terminated sections, then reveal the labels. Emit the complete LCS
alignment as transition indices and evidence references.

The positive holdout must be the unique highest-scoring epoch-1 section under
one fixed language, every template event must be aligned, and the event word
must contain at least two distinct ordered positions. Reverse the template
word as an order ablation; it must not tie the positive alignment unless the
reversal is byte-identical, in which case the language is non-discriminating.
Report action-word and section-length rankings as confuser baselines.

## Success criterion

At least one language has a nonempty, non-singleton template; aligns every
template event to the held-out completion; ranks that section uniquely above
all non-discharge boundaries with positive margin; preserves explicit
transition lineage; and beats the reversed-order ablation.

## Kill condition

The template has fewer than two events; no full ordered embedding exists; a
failure section ties or outranks the completion; reversal ties; only literal
operations, rendered coordinates, path length, or the hidden outcome label
separates the traces; or any event lacks a backward section.

## Claim boundary

A pass certifies only a prior-level option-mechanism transport. A separate
registered lowering is required before it can rank or execute a Level 3
intervention.
