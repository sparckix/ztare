# Success-anchored controllability reframe hypothesis

Date: 2026-07-26

Hypothesis ID: `H-ARC3-SUCCESS-ANCHORED-CONTROLLABILITY-20260726-24`

## Eigenquestion

Has acquisition stalled because its priority is defined over missing local
source-operation images rather than over transport toward task discharge?

## Left fingerprint

The learned interactive system is a compact partial-action graph: 130 nodes,
145 relations, four predictive contexts, six context transitions, ten typed
lifecycle boundaries, four operations, and zero ambiguity. Two distinct
operation pairs at the second epoch source terminate the lifecycle; the
boundary-first policy still ranks another 66-action sibling above a distinct
13-action ordinary frontier. The external task counter remains at two.

## Right fingerprint

Human action learning segments experience into events, associates consequences
with controllable actions, composes repeated action chunks, and selects
interventions relative to a desired consequence. Failure terminals constrain a
viability region, while successful prior episodes supply consequence anchors
for backward credit and transport.

## Hypothesis

A typed correspondence will identify the missing object as a
success-conditioned controllability or consequence potential over the partial
action graph. It will rank interventions by whether they discriminate or extend
a transport toward an externally witnessed success anchor, rather than by
boundary membership alone.

## Discriminating test

Run the existing meta-arc stall matcher, including its cross-cluster adversary,
then run `research_isomorphism` in conjecture mode with Codex over the two
fingerprints. Require explicit lowerings in both directions, mapped failure
modes, at least one prediction card, and a deterministic next discriminator
expressible using sealed graph/task receipts.

## Success criterion

The correspondence selects a typed carrier already present in the repository
or a narrowly missing invariant; distinguishes the 66-action boundary sibling
from the 13-action frontier or a prior-success transport; and states a
falsifiable consequence without using game-specific cells, colors, names, or
hand-authored dynamics.

## Kill condition

No candidate survives schema checks; the result is resemblance without
transport; failure modes are unmapped; the proposed discriminator cannot be
computed from sealed evidence; or the recommendation reduces to completing
every source-operation pair.

## Claim boundary

This is a representation-selection experiment. It neither certifies a carrier
nor establishes Level 3 completion.
