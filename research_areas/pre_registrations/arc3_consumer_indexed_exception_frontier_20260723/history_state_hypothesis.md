# History-bearing predictive state

Date: 2026-07-25

Parent tick: `tick-arc3-consumer-indexed-exception-frontier-20260723`

## Eigenquestion

What smallest evidence-derived history coordinate separates two identical
observations and operations whose consequences differ?

## Governing object

The candidate object is a predictive history state: an equivalence class of
observation-action histories under future operation consequences. Current
frame equality is one projection of that object, not its identity.

## Hypothesis

The newest non-commutation witness is separated by an existing generic
lifecycle coordinate—absolute/relative transition time, distance from the
last boundary, action-history suffix, or preceding mechanism-effect suffix.
The audit will compare the boundary history with every law-owned counterpart
sharing the identical frame and operation.

The smallest admissible lift must:

1. separate the named boundary and law consequences;
2. be computable from evidence available before the action;
3. improve determinism on all repeated observation-operation pairs, not only
   the named witness;
4. retain explicit boundary outcomes;
5. avoid task or substrate constants.

Time may enter only as an unquotiented coordinate or through a certified
translation/period relation. A raw step number is diagnostic, not a promoted
mechanism.

## Discriminating checks

1. Compare transition time, source/target epoch, distance since last boundary,
   and action/effect suffixes for the boundary row and law counterpart.
2. Enumerate repeated identical observation-operation pairs and measure
   consequence ambiguity under candidate history lifts.
3. Select the smallest lift that reduces ambiguity globally and separates the
   named pair.
4. Require an explicit mapping from history rows to lifted states and back to
   witnessed representatives.
5. Test frontier planning in the lifted relation without a live action.

## Kill conditions

Reject a lift if:

- it uses information observed only after the action;
- it memorizes one trace index;
- it separates the named pair but does not reduce global ambiguity;
- it erases a boundary or lifecycle transition;
- its equivalence claim lacks a future-consequence check.
