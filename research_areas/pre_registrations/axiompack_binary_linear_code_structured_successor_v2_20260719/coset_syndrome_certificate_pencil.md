## Eigenquestion

For the coordinate-zero shortening

\[
C\leq \mathbf F_2^{50},\qquad \dim C=19,\qquad d(C)=14,
\]

can the quotient be decided through its parity-check geometry, either by an
explicit syndrome whose leader has weight at least `14`, or by a replayable
certificate that every syndrome has a leader of weight at most `13`?

The discriminator must not repeat the previous candidate-center order. Its
unit of work is a syndrome or a global algebraic certificate, rather than an
outer proposal followed by a scan of all `2^19` codewords.

## Quotient/syndrome identity

The frozen canonical generator has systematic form

\[
G=[I_{19}\mid A].
\]

Use the systematic parity-check matrix

\[
H=[A^{\mathsf T}\mid I_{31}].
\]

For `x` in `F_2^50`, write `sigma(x)=Hx^T`. Then

\[
\ker\sigma=C,
\qquad
d(x,C)=\min\{\operatorname{wt}(e):He^{\mathsf T}=\sigma(x)\}.
\]

The prior gauge sets the first 19 coordinates to zero. Its 31 remaining bits
are therefore not merely quotient coordinates: under `H` they are the
syndrome itself. This supplies the exact compatibility map between the old
receipt and the new chart.

Let `ell(s)` be the least number of columns of `H` whose XOR is `s`. The
covering radius is

\[
\rho(C)=\max_{s\in\mathbf F_2^{31}}\ell(s).
\]

Thus:

- `ell(s) >= 14` gives a coset representative which, after complete adapter
  replay, yields a `[50,20,14]` extension;
- `rho(C) <= 13` excludes this one subcode-to-coset extension cone only.

## Ranked attack vectors and their counterattacks

### A. Dual-spectrum external-distance certificate

Enumerate the complete primal weight distribution `A_i` over the `2^19`
words of `C`. Recover the dual distribution exactly by MacWilliams:

\[
B_j=2^{-19}\sum_{i=0}^{50} A_i K_j(i).
\]

Let `s(C)` be the number of nonzero indices `j` for which `B_j` is nonzero.
The Delsarte external-distance bound gives `rho(C) <= s(C)`. The certificate
object is the frozen matrix digest, the complete primal and dual weight
distributions, exact Krawtchouk-transform replay, and the resulting support
count. If that count is at most 13, this is a compact global certificate.

Counterattack: a numerical transform or a partial spectrum can create false
zeros. Replay uses integers only, checks divisibility by `2^19`, nonnegative
dual multiplicities, total dual mass `2^31`, and the MacWilliams involution.
The binary-code adapter separately rechecks the frozen primal matrix.

Kill condition: `s(C) > 13`. This kills this proof object, not the syndrome
chart and not the extension cone.

### B. Subspace-chain decoder certificate

If the spectrum bound is too weak, seek a certificate in the Cayley graph of
the syndrome space generated by the 50 columns of `H`. A chain

\[
0=V_0<V_1<\cdots<V_m=\mathbf F_2^{31}
\]

may carry, at each quotient `V_i/V_{i-1}`, a frozen set of original columns
and a complete small table showing quotient diameter at most `r_i`. Lifting
the tables proves

\[
\rho(C)\leq\sum_i r_i.
\]

The certificate consists of the basis change, the original-column indices,
each finite quotient table, its predecessor edge, and a deterministic lift
replay. This exploits the five ten-coordinate quasicyclic blocks when they
actually compress the syndrome action; it does not assume that they do.

Counterattack: quotient tables can cover projections without giving a valid
lift in the full space. Replay must reconstruct each syndrome as an XOR of
the named original columns and count the combined support, with no reused
coordinate. A bound above 13 or a table whose size approaches all `2^31`
syndromes kills this certificate format as ineffective.

### C. Exact hostile syndrome

If neither upper-bound certificate closes, use the parity-check chart to seek
a syndrome outside the radius-13 ball image. A proposed syndrome earns credit
only after an independent exact minimization over the frozen `C` returns
leader weight at least 14, followed by the registered adapter on the appended
row. Solver `unknown`, a heuristic lower bound, or failure to find a decoder
does not make a witness.

Counterattack: the same syndrome can be presented under several parity-check
bases. Bind it both as a 31-bit systematic syndrome and as the canonical
50-bit representative, then replay `H v^T=s` and the full coset minimum.

### D. QC trellis / normalized-Viterbi certificate

The published source has the explicit `2 x 5` block presentation over

\[
R=\mathbf F_2[x]/(x^{10}-1).
\]

After coordinate-zero shortening, its message is `(u,v)` with `u in R`,
`u_0=0`, and `v in R`: exactly 19 bits. At the block cut after the two
systematic blocks, the conventional code-trellis separator is this message
pair. Equivalently, for any coordinate prefix `P` and suffix `Q`, the minimal
trellis state dimension is

\[
s(P,Q)=\operatorname{rank}(G_P)+\operatorname{rank}(G_Q)-19.
\]

The QC block order reaches `s=19`, hence at most `2^19` conventional states.
A normalized Viterbi state at a cut is the vector of least prefix distances
to those separator states, modulo addition of a common constant. Its exact
transition on received bit `b` is

\[
m'(z')=\min_{z\xrightarrow{c}z'}(m(z)+[b\ne c]),
\qquad
\mu'=m'-\min m'.
\]

The removed minimum is the transition payment. A finite transition graph
whose every length-50 path has total payment at most 13 would certify
`rho(C)<=13`; the replay object would list the separator identity, every
reachable normalized metric state, both bit transitions, payments, and the
terminal maximum.

There is an early category-kill check. Write a received word as systematic
prefix `y` plus the 31 remaining bits. Translating by the unique codeword
with message `y` sends the prefix to zero and sends the suffix to its residual
syndrome `q`. Distance to `C` is invariant under this translation. Therefore
normalizing the 19-bit separator recovers exactly the earlier carrier

\[
q\in R^3\oplus\mathbf F_2\cong\mathbf F_2^{31}.
\]

Unless the QC action supplies a further certified orbit quotient, the metric
automaton has merely renamed the full syndrome chart. Kill the trellis lane
before construction if this rank-31 residual survives, or if state vectors or
transition receipts must be expanded per syndrome. A bound of `2^19` on the
conventional separator alone does not count as compression of the received
residual.

### E. Proof-producing low-message majority cover

For `t <= 4`, freeze the message ball

\[
U_t=\{u\in\mathbf F_2^{19}:\operatorname{wt}(u)\leq t\}.
\]

Its cardinalities are respectively `1, 20, 191, 1160, 5036` for
`t=0,1,2,3,4`. These centers are selected before any solver outcome. They
cover the syndrome cube at radius 13 exactly when the Boolean formula

\[
F_t(q)=\bigwedge_{u\in U_t}
  [\operatorname{wt}(q+uA)\geq 14-\operatorname{wt}(u)]
\]

is unsatisfiable. Any satisfying `q` is replayed over the complete row span;
it only kills that value of `t`.

The preferred negative proof carrier reuses the repository's existing
explicit-LRAT path. An equivalent support formulation has 50 Boolean
variables `x_i`, exact weight 14, and for every retained codeword `c` the
majority inequality

\[
\sum_{i\in\operatorname{supp}(c)}x_i\leq\operatorname{wt}(c)/2.
\]

Its CNF must use a frozen, model-replayed cardinality encoding. CaDiCaL may
produce LRAT, but the terminal object is accepted only if
`Mathlib.Tactic.Sat.FromLRAT.lrat_proof` constructs the explicit proof term,
the axiom audit stays within the current allowlist, a tampered retained hint
fails, and a separate Lean bridge proves that CNF satisfiability is equivalent
to a weight-14 leader satisfying the named original-column inequalities.

The exact size estimate precedes solving. A weight-14 support covers only
`0.7107%` of all 14-subsets, so any certificate made solely of such supports
needs at least 141 original inequalities by counting; the complete first
class contains 1,155. Freeze viability caps of `100,000` CNF variables,

Lean construction. These are stricter than the prior 92 MB trace which failed

## Proof/certificate objects

The admissible terminal objects are exactly:

1. `syndrome_witness`: frozen `H`, syndrome, canonical representative,
   exhaustive coset-minimum receipt, rank-20 generator, and registered
   distance receipt; or
2. `covering_upper_certificate`: frozen `H` and a globally replayable proof
   object establishing `ell(s)<=13` for every one of the `2^31` syndromes.

A solver status without one of these objects is `unavailable`. The prior
center constraints may be used as hostile tests, but not as proof premises
for a global conclusion.

## Exact success and kill conditions

Success is either:

- one exact coset leader of weight at least 14, adapter-certified after
  adjoining its representative; or
- a global certificate with a deterministic replay establishing covering
  radius at most 13 for this byte-frozen shortening.

Kill this discriminator if all candidate certificates require essentially
enumerating the full 31-bit quotient without compression, or if their replay
depends on floating point, sampled words, generated executable code, or an
uncertified solver `UNSAT`. Such a kill selects a new representation; it does
not imply that the target code is absent.
