# AxiomPack inverse-semigroup portability smoke

Prepared 2026-07-10 before campaign construction or model dispatch.

## Hypothesis

Within a frozen 20-minute envelope, the signature-generic AxiomPack path can
map the short-equation landscape of finite inverse semigroups and either:

1. freeze an anonymous exact-two presentation with a nonzero consequence
   residual, plus a concrete base-relative countermodel to the target under
   each singleton premise; or
2. emit a receipted no-candidate result showing that the declared formula/model
   band is too compressed for this search.

The positive branch must then nominate a target that returns either a larger
finite countermodel or a governed conditional Lean result.

## Eigenquestion

Does the portable formula–model kernel expose compound identity structure in a
unary-binary algebraic variety without a family-specific adapter, or does the
short exact band collapse into base consequences and cheap rewrites?

## Frozen substrate

The campaign uses `generic_fol_finite.v1`, one anonymous binary operation, one
anonymous unary operation, and the three-equation basis from Araújo and Kinyon,
*An elegant 3-basis for inverse semigroups*:

- `x = (x x') x`;
- `(x x') (y' y) = (y' y) (x x')`;
- `(x y) z = x (y z'')`.

The source proves that this type-(2,1) basis characterizes inverse semigroups
and that the unary operation becomes natural inversion:
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4028>.

Candidate formulas are every canonical universal equation through total
operation order two. The exact context contains all size-two and size-three
interpretations satisfying the base, quotiented by sort-preserving
isomorphism with labeled multiplicities retained. Interpretation names and
literature are withheld from the navigator.

## Discriminating test

Run `campaign.md` through the named Hetzner `leanmill-campaign` action using
Codex gpt-5.5 low via the existing subscription runtime. Do not run the
campaign on the workstation. If a finalist freezes, run only its selected
size-four/five SMT and single conditional Lean boundary under the same budget.
Literature comparison begins only after freeze and verification.

Classify the first binding constraint:

1. context has too few semantic profiles or nodes: representation band too
   narrow;
2. no receipted finalist: navigation or endogenous-interest null;
3. target loses a singleton countermodel: candidate is not logically joint;
4. pair target has a size-four/five countermodel: small-context instability;
5. no larger countermodel and Lean unresolved: formal-lift bottleneck;
6. governed proof lacks logical or proof attribution: causal failure;
7. attributed proof maps immediately to a standard identity: useful control,
   not a discovery;
8. attributed, source-unmapped mechanism: candidate for a larger campaign, not
   a novelty claim.

## Success criterion

The apparatus criterion is met if the campaign completes without a
family-specific module, every frozen candidate carries positive residual
coordinates and two host-replayed singleton countermodels, and at least one
selected boundary returns a concrete larger model or a governed proof result.

Scientific escalation requires more: the mechanism must survive bounded
post-freeze source review and motivate a sharper next experiment. A failed
search is not evidence of novelty.

## Kill conditions

- the compiler or runner requires an inverse-semigroup adapter;
- a base equation or empty-theory consequence enters residual credit;
- relabeled copies are counted as distinct theory evidence;
- a target lacks one of the claimed singleton countermodels;
- absence of a fixed-size countermodel is promoted to proof;
- the navigator is forced to nominate after residual collapse;
- a known identity is described as new because its notation was anonymous;
- the distributed-systems application claim is used as mathematical evidence.

## Claim boundary

This smoke tests portability, search geometry, and causal boundary wiring in an
active identity-theory domain. It cannot establish novelty, depth, practical
distributed-systems value, or superiority over existing automated-deduction
systems.
