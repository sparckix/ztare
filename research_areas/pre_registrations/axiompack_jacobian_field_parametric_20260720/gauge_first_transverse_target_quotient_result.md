## Graded coordinates

Let

\[
R=\mathbb Q[[s]][y,g],
\qquad
D=f(y,g)\partial_y+n(y,g)\partial_g,
\qquad n\in(g).
\]

Write

\[
f=\sum_{m\ge0}g^mf_m(y),
\qquad
n=\sum_{m\ge0}g^{m+1}n_{m+1}(y).
\]

The weighted-volume equation

\[
\partial_y(g^2f)+\partial_g(g^2n)=0
\]

gives

\[
n_{m+1}=-\frac{f_m'}{m+3}.
\]

Thus a homogeneous layer is represented by

\[
D_f^{(m)}
=g^mf\partial_y-\frac{g^{m+1}f'}{m+3}\partial_g.
\]

Direct calculation gives the graded bracket

\[
[D_f^{(m)},D_h^{(n)}]
=D_{B_{m,n}(f,h)}^{(m+n)},
\]

\[
B_{m,n}(f,h)
=(m+n+3)
\left(
\frac{fh'}{n+3}-\frac{hf'}{m+3}
\right).
\]

This coordinate law explains both the truncated first-neighborhood closure
and the first possible secondary obstruction.

## First-neighborhood Lie closure

Modulo \(g^2\), put

\[
T=\partial_y,\qquad
Y=gy\partial_y,\qquad
C=g\partial_y.
\]

Then

\[
[T,Y]=C,\qquad [T,C]=[Y,C]=0
\quad\bmod g^2.
\]

Every first-neighborhood Magnus coefficient remains in
\(\langle T,Y,C\rangle\), with transverse \(y\)-degree at most one.
For the full graded completion,

\[

=-\frac54D_1^{(2)},

so the finite truncated closure does not imply a bounded global logarithm.
