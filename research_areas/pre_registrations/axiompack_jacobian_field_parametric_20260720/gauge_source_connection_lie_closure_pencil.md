# Lie closure of the regular source connection

**Status:** closed — complete target image computed; source-only divisor
cascade removed by a regular target gauge

## Eigenquestion

The normalized family has the exact regular source-only infinitesimal
connection

\[
\partial_sF_s=dF_s\,V_s,
\qquad
\deg_{v,t}V_s=11.
\]

Does its path-ordered flow admit a logarithm whose coefficient vector fields
remain in one fixed polynomial-degree space?

This is the first discriminator that can separate the source-map escape
theorem from logarithmic-generator escape.  Repeated composition of a
bounded-degree vector field can make the assembled map coefficients grow.

## Finite coefficient system

Write the rational connection over a common scalar denominator as

\[
V_s=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{r}s^iZ_i}{d(s)},
\qquad Z_i\in\operatorname{Der}\mathbb Q[v,t].
\]

The first test is the Lie algebra generated by the coefficient fields
\(\{Z_i\}\).

- If their exact bracket closure is finite-dimensional, every Magnus
  coefficient belongs to that fixed space.  This supplies an all-order
  bounded-degree logarithmic contact and kills logarithmic escape.
- If the closure produces fields of increasing degree, that only identifies
  a possible escape mechanism.  A lower theorem additionally needs to show
  that the corresponding brackets occur with nonzero Magnus coefficients
  for this specific rational path.

## Discriminating computation

1. Extract a primitive common denominator \(d(s)\) and the independent
   coefficient fields \(Z_i\).
2. Compute exact brackets and vector-space ranks degree by degree.
3. Compute the Magnus logarithm directly through the first order at which a
   new bracket shell can occur.
4. Compare its top homogeneous shell with the free-Lie prediction.

The calculation supports bounded logarithmic complexity only if bracket
closure is proved, rather than merely observed through a cutoff.  It supports
unbounded complexity only if a nonzero all-order family of Magnus shells or
an invariant recurrence is proved.  Finite growth data alone remains a
prefix result.

## Kill conditions

The lane is killed by any of:

- the displayed degree-11 connection having a parameter pole at \(s=0\);
- the extracted source field leaving either lift ideal;
- scalar-denominator normalization changing the generated Lie algebra;
- apparent bracket growth whose Magnus coefficient vanishes;
- or a bounded-degree change of source gauge that conjugates the connection
  into a finite-dimensional Lie algebra.

## Intended formal surface

A promotable result will expose one of two finite kernels:

- a bracket-closure table plus the standard Magnus-in-a-Lie-subalgebra
  implication; or
- a recurrence for a nonzero associated-graded Magnus coefficient whose
  degree tends to infinity.

The ambient connection-to-contact integration remains a separately checked
formal-series bridge.

## Exceptional-divisor reduction

The first bracket pass rules out finite-dimensional closure: all 55 brackets
between the eleven independent coefficient fields leave their degree-11
span.  A smaller all-order discriminator is available on the invariant
exceptional divisor \(\gamma=0\).

The restricted connection has the form

\[
\overline V_s=f_s(v)\partial_v,
\qquad \deg_v f_s=3,
\]

with scalar denominators regular at \(s=0\).  Write

\[
f_s=f_0+s f_1+s^2f_2+\cdots
\]

and \(A=f_0\partial_v\), \(B=f_1\partial_v\).  The candidate mechanism is:

1. in the Witt coordinate \(y=2v+3\), the first three fields are
   \[
   A=\frac{y^3}{6}\partial_y,\quad
   B=\frac{7y^3-9y+10}{48}\partial_y,\quad
   C=\frac{y^2(8y+15)}{288}\partial_y;
   \]
2. if \(R_k=\operatorname{ad}_A^kB\), then
   \(\deg R_1=3\) and \(\deg R_k=2k\) for \(k\ge2\);
3. at the top Witt index,
   \[
   \operatorname{ad}_A^{k-1}C
   =-\frac12\operatorname{ad}_A^kB
   \text{ lower degree}\qquad(k\ge2);
   \]
4. the universal linearized Magnus functions \(\phi_1,\phi_2\) obey
   \[
   \phi_1(x)-\frac{x}{2}\phi_2(x)
   =\frac{x}{2(e^x-1)};
   \]
5. for even \(k\ge2\), the resulting coefficient is
   \(B_k/(2k!)\ne0\); equivalently, the even coefficients satisfy the
   alternating positive convolution recurrence obtained from
   \(x h'=h-h^2+x^2/4\) for
   \(h=(x/2)\coth(x/2)\);
6. Witt-index efficiency makes every word with at least two
   positive-order fields, or one \(f_j\) with \(j\ge3\), strictly lower
   than this shell.

If all six statements survive exact replay, then the source-only
logarithm has

\[
\deg_v\!\left(Y_n|_{\gamma=0}\right)=2n-4
\quad\text{for every even }n\ge4.
\]

This would prove unbounded logarithmic complexity in the source-only gauge.
It would not settle minimization over target Hamiltonian gauges: the
normalized first-order target field removes \(A\), so the Bernoulli cascade
is precisely one of the shells a target gauge can cancel.

## Target-control quotient on the exceptional divisor

**Status:** pre-computation pencil

The next eigenquestion is whether the unbounded Witt shells above survive
after quotienting by every regular polynomial Hamiltonian target gauge.
For an instantaneous target Hamiltonian \(K_s(P,Q)\), put

\[
X_{K_s}=(\partial_QK_s,-\partial_PK_s),\qquad
W_s=(dF_s)^{-1}\bigl(X_{K_s}\circ F_s\bigr).
\]

Only choices for which \(W_s\) is polynomial and belongs to the admissible
equivariant source category may act as gauges.  The calculation must impose
that condition before restricting to \(\gamma=0\); a rational pullback whose
restriction happens to be polynomial is not an admissible control.

The determinant identity supplies the structural constraint.  The target
contact preserves ordinary area, while the quotient source contact preserves
the weighted area form \(\gamma^2\,dv\wedge dt\).  At group level,

\[
(\gamma\circ\Psi_s)^2\det D\Psi_s=\gamma^2.
\]

Infinitesimally,

\[
\operatorname{div}W+2\,\frac{W(\gamma)}{\gamma}=0.
\]

For a polynomial admissible field this forces
\(W(\gamma)\in(\gamma)\).  Thus every admissible source gauge is tangent to
\(\gamma=0\), and its restriction is a one-variable polynomial field in
\(y=2v+3\).  It need not fix \(\gamma\) pointwise; imposing
\(W(\gamma)=0\) would exclude the normalized first target control and define
a narrower category than the campaign.

### Competing mechanisms

1. **Finite control image.**  Reduce a generic polynomial Hamiltonian in the
   canonical normal form
   \[
   K=A(P,C)+QB(P,C)
   \]
   and pull its field through the fixed inverse cubic.  If polynomial
   descent and \(\gamma\)-tangency force the divisor restriction into a
   fixed finite-dimensional Witt subspace, quotient the connection by that
   subspace and test whether an infinite even shell remains.

2. **Surjective shell control.**  If admissible Hamiltonians produce
   arbitrarily high Witt modes on the divisor, determine their parameter
   cost and filtered degree.  A control that cancels an order-\(n\) source
   shell only by introducing an equally large target shell kills a
   source-only lower bound but may preserve the symmetric contact-complexity
   lower bound.

3. **Pure-gauge trivialization.**  A one-dimensional formal connection can
   be gauge-trivial as an abstract vector-field connection.  This kills the
   route unless the trivializing field fails polynomial descent, the lift
   ideals, regularity at \(s=0\), or the target Hamiltonian filtration.

4. **Moving-divisor artifact.**  If a proposed target correction gives a
   nonzero normal component on \(\gamma=0\), it is outside the declared
   volume-preserving contact category.  It cannot be used to cancel the
   Witt cascade.

### Discriminating calculation

1. Compute the exact restriction map
   \[
   K\longmapsto
   \left.(dF_0)^{-1}(X_K\circ F_0)\right|_{\gamma=0}
   \]
   after enforcing polynomial pullback and \(\gamma\)-tangency.
2. Express its image in the Witt basis and identify the kernel and cokernel
   by exact rank or module reduction.
3. Repeat coefficientwise for a regular \(s\)-adic target Hamiltonian and
   record the parameter-order cost of each attainable Witt index.
4. Transform the first three source-only fields \(A,B,C\) by the complete
   admissible control image.  Either exhibit a residual leading chain with a
   nonzero all-order Magnus coefficient, or exhibit a compatible target
   gauge that removes every such chain.

### Success and kill conditions

A gauge-independent logarithmic escape claim requires a quotient class whose
Witt index tends to infinity and whose coefficient is proved nonzero at an
infinite set of orders.  Finite rank growth is insufficient.

The lane is killed if the complete admissible target image cancels the
source-only connection on the divisor with regular polynomial controls, or
if every proposed residual depends on the source-only slice.  In that case
the surviving campaign question returns to the complete fixed-cutoff
minimax \(c_n\) and to symmetric source/target filtration rather than this
divisor cascade.

## Post-divisor global normal-form discriminator

The first exact target-control calculation produced the regular coefficient

\[
a(s)=
-\frac{192(s^2-3s-8)}
{(s-6)^3(s-4)^2(s+4)^2}
\]

for \(K_s=a(s)P^3\).  Its controlled restriction is affine:

\[
\left.V_s^{\mathrm{controlled}}\right|_{\gamma=0}
=
\frac{
s(9s^2y-15s^2-144y+160)
}{
3(s-4)^2(s+4)^2
}\partial_y.
\]

This kills the divisor lower-bound mechanism.  It also defines a canonical
global connection whose spatial numerator degree is finite.  The next
eigenquestion is whether that global connection belongs to a
finite-dimensional polynomial Lie algebra, or whether its brackets escape
away from the divisor.

The discriminating test is:

1. allow the complete lowest-weight target controls \(P^3\) and \(PQ\);
2. first solve exactly for a regular divisor normal form (affine, and

3. extract the independent coefficient fields of the resulting global

4. compute exact bracket closure and the first Magnus shell that leaves the

A finite bracket table gives a uniformly bounded source logarithm and would
close the global bounded-contact lane after integration checks.  Bracket
escape is informative only if the escaping shell survives the remaining
target controls; otherwise it is another gauge artifact.  A failure confined
to \(\gamma\)-multiples identifies the next exceptional layer to study.

### Pencil reduction before exact replay

Write \(r=1+v\), \(g=\gamma\), and \(y=2r+1=2v+3\).  At the seed,

\[
P=yg-\frac34(y-1)^2g^2,\qquad
Q=\frac14(y^2-1)g^2-\frac14(y-1)^3g^3,
\]

and

\[
\det\frac{\partial(P,Q)}{\partial(r,g)}=-g^2.
\]

The target lift ideals for
\[
X_K=(K_Q,-K_P)
\]
remove the \(P,Q,P^2\) terms of \(K\).  With
\(\operatorname{wt}(P,Q)=(1,2)\), every nonconstant surviving monomial
therefore has weight at least three.  After multiplication by the adjugate
Jacobian and division by \(g^2\), only weight-three Hamiltonians can have a
nonzero divisor restriction.  They are

\[
P^3,\qquad PQ.
\]

The predicted restriction table is

\[
\begin{aligned}
P^3&\longmapsto -6y^3\partial_y,\\
PQ&\longmapsto
-\frac32(y^3-y)\partial_y.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the candidate complete seed image is

\[
\left\langle y\partial_y,y^3\partial_y\right\rangle.
\]

The \(C\)-normal form supplies an independent exhaustiveness check.  If
\(K=A(P,C)+QB(P,C)\), the three target-lift conditions at the origin force

\[
A_P(0,0)=0,\qquad
A_{PP}(0,0)=2A_C(0,0),\qquad
B(0,0)=-4A_C(0,0).
\]

Hence its apparently lower-weight part is actually

\[
A_C(0,0)(C+P^2-4Q)
=A_C(0,0)(4P^3-18PQ+27Q^2),
\]

again leaving exactly the two weight-three directions above.

There is one determinant correction to test explicitly.  A polynomial
pullback of a Hamiltonian field preserves the pulled-back area form
\(-g^2\,dr\wedge dg\), so its infinitesimal identity is

\[
\partial_r(g^2W_r)+\partial_g(g^2W_g)=0.
\]

This implies \(W_g\in(g)\), hence preservation of the divisor ideal.  It
does not imply \(W_g=0\) away from the divisor.  Requiring the coordinate
\(g\) itself to be fixed would define a smaller category and would exclude
the normalized first target gauge.  The replay must distinguish these two
conditions rather than infer pointwise \(g\)-fixing from volume
preservation.

The sharp kill test is stronger than quotienting only \(A,B,C\) at the
seed.  For the full rational family, search for regular functions \(a(s)\)
and \(b(s)\) such that

\[
V_s-a(s)(dF_s)^{-1}X_{P^3}(F_s)
-b(s)(dF_s)^{-1}X_{PQ}(F_s)
\]

restricts to a fixed finite-dimensional Lie algebra.  If it restricts to
translations or projective vector fields for all \(s\), the Bernoulli--Witt
cascade is removed by a compatible target gauge and this divisor lane is
killed.

### Exact outcome

The seed image is exactly

\[
\left\langle y\partial_y,y^3\partial_y\right\rangle.
\]

The regular control

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(s)&=
\frac{96(s^2-12s+16)}
{(s-6)^3(s-4)^2(s+4)^2},\\
b(s)&=\frac{2s}{(s-4)(s+4)}
\end{aligned}
\]

gives

\[
\left.
\left(
V_s-a(s)(dF_s)^{-1}X_{P^3}(F_s)
-b(s)(dF_s)^{-1}X_{PQ}(F_s)
\right)
\right|_{\gamma=0}
=
\frac{160s}{3(s-4)^2(s+4)^2}\partial_y.
\]

Since \(Q^2\) is in the divisor kernel for every \(s\), adding
\(-Q^2/4\) makes the control at \(s=0\) equal to the campaign's normalized
first target Hamiltonian
\(-P^3/36-Q^2/4\), without changing the translation identity.

The kill condition therefore fires.  The complete calculation and category

## First transverse \(\gamma\)-adic quotient

**Status:** pre-computation pencil

The divisor normal form leaves a translation-valued connection, while the
global coefficient fields still have growing brackets.  The next
eigenquestion is whether that growth survives in the first transverse
quotient after every admissible target control has been removed.

Work over

\[
R=\mathbb Q[[s]][y,g],\qquad g=\gamma.
\]

Every admissible source field tangent to the divisor can be written

\[
D=f(y,g)\partial_y+n(y,g)\partial_g,
\qquad n\in(g).
\]

Write

\[
f=\sum_{m\ge0}g^mf_m(y),
\qquad
n=\sum_{m\ge0}g^{m+1}n_{m+1}(y).
\]

The weighted-volume equation

\[
\partial_y(g^2f)+\partial_g(g^2n)=0
\]

gives the coefficient identity

\[
n_{m+1}=-\frac{f_m'}{m+3}.
\]

Thus the degree-\(m\) associated-graded field is determined by one
polynomial:

\[
D_f^{(m)}
=g^mf(y)\partial_y
-\frac{g^{m+1}f'(y)}{m+3}\partial_g.
\]

Its bracket is

\[
[D_f^{(m)},D_h^{(n)}]
=D_{B_{m,n}(f,h)}^{(m+n)},
\qquad
B_{m,n}(f,h)
=(m+n+3)
\left(
\frac{fh'}{n+3}-\frac{hf'}{m+3}
\right).
\]

In particular, the divisor translation acts on layer one by

\[
B_{0,1}(c,f)=cf',
\]

and two layer-one fields bracket into layer two.  The first transverse jet
is

\[
J_1(D)=
\left(f_1,-\frac14f_1'\right),
\]

using the \(g\partial_y\) and \(g^2\partial_g\) coefficients.  This indexing
retains the normal data paired with the tangential jet; the
\(g\partial_g\) coefficient belongs to the divisor layer and equals
\(-f_0'/3\).

### Complete target vocabulary at this layer

The normal weights are

\[
\operatorname{wt}(P,Q)=(1,2).
\]

The two weight-three controls \(P^3,PQ\) are already uniquely determined by
the requirement that the divisor connection be translation-valued: their
divisor image has rank two.  A Hamiltonian of weight at least five acts only
at order \(g^2\) or higher in the tangential component.  Therefore the
complete new target image in \(J_1\) is generated by the three weight-four
monomials

\[
P^4,\qquad P^2Q,\qquad Q^2.
\]

The coefficient of \(Q^2\) already has constant term \(-1/4\) in the
normalized first slice.  Further weight-four controls must preserve the
fixed slice: their added coefficients vanish at \(s=0\).  No restriction is
to be imposed before polynomial pullback, both source lift ideals, weighted
divergence, and divisor tangency have been checked.

### Discriminating calculation

1. Extract \(J_1\) for the normalized full connection and for the three
   weight-four target pullbacks.
2. Compute the exact target-image rank over \(\mathbb Q(s)\), its kernel,
   and a canonical cokernel representative.
3. Solve for controls regular at \(s=0\) whose added coefficients vanish
   there.  Record separately any reduction available only over
   \(\mathbb Q(s)\) with a pole at the seed.
4. Form the induced parameter connection in the quotient and compute its
   first brackets and Magnus coefficients.
5. If growth survives, identify an all-order associated-graded recurrence;
   if it does not, advance to the next \(g\)-adic layer rather than infer
   global boundedness.

### Success and kill conditions

A first-layer lower mechanism requires a nonzero quotient class whose
polynomial degree grows through an infinite, proved family and which cannot
be removed by regular fixed-slice controls.  A finite prefix of bracket
degrees is insufficient.

The layer-one route is killed if the normalized jet belongs to the complete
regular target image, or if its quotient connection is conjugate into a
fixed finite-dimensional Lie algebra.  Either outcome is useful: it
separates a gauge artifact from the first transverse obstruction and
specifies the next layer without changing the contact category.

## All-layer weighted target image

**Status:** pencil theorem extracted from the layer-zero and layer-one
calculations

The two completed layers suggest one uniform mechanism.  Write the leading
exceptional expansion as

\[
P=gA(y)+O(g^2),\qquad Q=g^2D(y)+O(g^3),
\]

with

\[
AD'-2A'D=\frac12.
\]

Let \(H(P,Q)\) be weighted homogeneous of degree \(d\ge3\), for
\(\operatorname{wt}(P,Q)=(1,2)\), and put \(m=d-3\).  The leading pullback
of its Hamiltonian field must have the volume-preserving form

\[
D_f^{(m)}
=g^mf\partial_y-\frac{g^{m+1}f'}{m+3}\partial_g.
\]

Comparing the leading \(P\)- and \(Q\)-components gives

\[
A'f-\frac{A}{d}f'=H_Q(A,D),
\qquad
D'f-\frac{2D}{d}f'=-H_P(A,D).
\]

Weighted Euler elimination and the determinant identity predict the exact
formula

\[
\boxed{f=-2d\,H(A,D).}
\]

This specializes to the already replayed divisor fields
\(-6A^3\) and \(-6AD\).

Since \(A=ay+b\) is affine with \(a\) a unit at the seed, the determinant
identity also gives

\[
D=\lambda A^2-\frac1{4a}.
\]

Consequently the complete weight-\(d\) target image is

\[
\mathcal C_m
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
A^{d-2j}D^j:
0\le j\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor
\right\}
=
A^{d\bmod2}\,
\mathbb Q[[s]][A^2]_{\le d}.
\]

Thus it is the full parity-\(d\) polynomial subspace in the affine
coordinate \(A\), with unit triangular determinant.  Every weighted
monomial of degree \(d\ge3\) satisfies the target lift ideals.  Higher
weights begin at later \(g\)-layers, and injectivity of each displayed
image removes hidden lower-weight freedom once earlier layers have been
normalized.

### Recursive normal-form question

At layer \(m\), project the connection coefficient \(f_m\) onto
\(\mathcal C_m\) and retain the opposite-parity representative.  The
projection coefficients should be regular and seed-zero whenever the
normalized connection vanishes at \(s=0\).

The decisive question is whether these opposite-parity residues stay in a
fixed-degree graded Lie algebra or generate nonzero classes in arbitrarily
high layers.  The first two residues are

\[
f_0=c(s),\qquad
f_1=-\frac{16s}{(s+4)^3}y.
\]

Their completed fields satisfy

\[
[D_1^{(0)},D_y^{(1)}]=D_1^{(1)},\qquad
[D_y^{(1)},D_1^{(m)}]=-\frac{m+4}{4}D_1^{(m+1)}.
\]

Odd-layer constants belong to the target image, while even-layer constants
survive its parity quotient.  This makes the first possible secondary
obstruction a layer-two constant.  It must be computed in the full
connection: brackets involving target-exact lower-layer terms and native
higher-layer coefficients can either create or cancel it.

The lane succeeds only with a nonzero all-order sequence of canonical
opposite-parity residues or a bounded-degree construction for the coupled
source and target logarithms.  The parity image theorem alone is a normal
form, not an asymptotic conclusion.

## Hamiltonian compression and the asymptotic eigenquestion

**Status:** preregistered before the recursive all-layer calculation

The vector-field recursion has a scalar formulation.  In the exceptional
coordinates the quotient Jacobian is

\[
\det d(P,Q)=-\frac{g^2}{2}.
\]

If \(X_H=(H_Q,-H_P)\) is a target Hamiltonian field, its pullback is the
source Hamiltonian field for

\[
\mathcal H_{\rm src}=-2H(P,Q)
\]

with respect to \(g^2\,dy\wedge dg\).  Likewise, the source-only parameter
connection has a scalar Hamiltonian \(L_s\), determined by

\[
\partial_gL_s=g^2W_s(y),\qquad
\partial_yL_s=-g^2W_s(g).
\]

Thus the recursive target normalization is triangular scalar reduction of
\(L_s+2K_s(P,Q)\), rather than a fresh inverse-Jacobian calculation at every
weight.

### Eigenquestion

Let \(R_m(A)\) be the opposite-parity representative after normalizing
layers below \(m\), and put

\[
r_m=[A^m]R_m.
\]

Does the coefficient of \(s\) in \(r_m\) satisfy a closed recurrence with
infinitely many nonzero terms?  If so, does that sequence survive the
*symmetric* source/target filtration, rather than only the greedy target
normalization?

The graded bracket lowers total \(A\)-degree by one.  Consequently, under
the conditional bound \(\deg R_j\leq j\), every bracket word assembled from
lower layers has \(A\)-degree at most \(m-1\) in layer \(m\).  This makes
\([A^m]R_m\) immune to lower-layer Magnus words.  It does not by itself make
the greedy normal form a minimax invariant: a bounded representative may
retain target-removable lower coefficients instead of applying the
canonical projection.

### Discriminating calculation

1. Derive \(L_s\) and replay the scalar pullback identity against the
   vector-field implementation.
2. Linearize at \(s=0\).  Solve the triangular normal form over
   \(\mathbb Q[[g]][A]\), recording \(r_m^{(1)}=[sA^m]R_m\) and the
   \(s\)-valuation of every target control.
3. Infer a candidate recurrence only from exact terms, then prove it from
   the scalar substitution law or a rational generating function.
4. Compare with the complete \(C\)-normal target module and the shifted
   Rees source filtration.  State the exact implication for
   \(\sigma_{\rm ct}\); do not identify canonical growth with a symmetric
   lower bound without this comparison.

### Success and kill conditions

The unboundedness lane succeeds only if all three statements hold:

- \(r_m^{(1)}\neq0\) for infinitely many \(m\);
- lower-layer BCH words cannot alter its top \(A\)-coefficient;
- any representative with both source and target degree bounded would have
  the same eventually nonzero coefficient, or an equivalent filtered
  obstruction.

It is killed if the sequence terminates, if a finite polynomial target
Hamiltonian gives a bounded representative, or if the canonical chain is
created solely by the chosen projection.  A sequence of regular controls
whose \(s\)-valuations stay bounded is not an admissible element of
\(\mathbb Q[P,Q][[s]]\): every coefficient of \(s^k\) must contain only
finitely many target monomials.

### Exact layer-two outcome

The first secondary class survives.  The complete weight-five target image
has rank three, while adjoining the normalized layer-two residual raises
the rank to four.  In the affine exceptional coordinate \(A(s,y)\), its
canonical opposite-parity representative is nonzero and begins

\[
[s]R_2^{\mathrm{even}}(s,A)
=\frac{31-45A^2}{96}.
\]

The scalar replay and full rational representative are recorded in
[`gauge_second_transverse_target_quotient_result.md`](gauge_second_transverse_target_quotient_result.md).
This discharges the first bullet only at layer two.  The recurrence and
the minimax comparison remain the active gates.

### Ex-post correction to the defect shortcut

The termwise claim that target corrections of defect at least four cannot
feed defect three is false.  Already the trace and canonical weight-four
representatives differ by

\[
\frac56\left(Q-\frac{P^2}{4}\right)^2,
\]

and after seed pullback its weight-six \(A^3\) coefficient is
\(-35/192\).  Thus an individual defect-four correction can contribute to
the defect-three shell.

The recurrence survives for a different reason.  In adapted cusp
coordinates \((\xi,N)\), with \(p(\xi)=P\) and \(N=Q-q(\xi)\), the complete
target jet through defect three is triangular:

\[
H_0(\xi)+N H_1(\xi),
\qquad N^2=O(g^4).
\]

Computing the whole shell in these coordinates includes the cascading
corrections and their cancellations.  The resulting rational generating
function, rather than termwise defect monotonicity, proves the canonical
top recurrence.

## Ex-post Rees polar-prefix correction

The exact regular source-only connection itself disproves the proposed
shortcut that weighted divergence might annihilate every polar source
cascade.  Put

\[
s=\tau\varepsilon^2,\qquad
(v,t)=(V/\varepsilon,T/\varepsilon),
\qquad
U_\varepsilon
=\varepsilon^3V_{\tau\varepsilon^2}(V/\varepsilon,T/\varepsilon).
\]

Direct replay checks

\[
\partial_v(\gamma^2V_{s,v})
+\partial_t(\gamma^2V_{s,t})=0
\]

and the exact scaled contact identity

\[
\partial_\tau\widetilde F_{\varepsilon,\tau}
=D\widetilde F_{\varepsilon,\tau}\,U_\varepsilon.
\]

The field \(U_\varepsilon\) has a nonzero
\(\varepsilon^{-6}\) principal part.  Writing
\(G=T-\frac32V\) and \(r=VG\), it is

\[
\begin{aligned}
[\,\varepsilon^{-6}\,]U_{\varepsilon,V}
&=-\frac34V^6G^3(\tau r-2),\\
[\,\varepsilon^{-6}\,]U_{\varepsilon,T}
&=\frac34V^5G^3(T-3V)(\tau r-2).
\end{aligned}
\]

It preserves the leading weighted area density:

\[
\partial_V(G^2U_{-6,V})
+\partial_T(G^2U_{-6,T})=0.
\]
