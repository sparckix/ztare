# Pure contact-zero finite-prefix adversary

## Outcome

The finite radial-prefix lane has an exact all-index Newton-face identity,
but the proposed unconditional payment transition is false in the complete
contact-zero coefficient complex.  Two coupled effects intervene:

1. the radial source letter carries a first-normal tangent companion, and
   the complete defect-one face cancels identically; and
2. even when the transported velocity has an infinite radial-adjoint tail,
   typed forward-`dexp` can package it into a finite logarithm.

Thus the radial identity below is a conditional actor lemma, not an
unconditional source-logarithm rate witness.

After the fixed normalized row-zero target Hamiltonian

\[
K_0=-\frac1{36}P^3-\frac14Q^2,
\]

the complete source Hamiltonian has zero row zero.  Its row-one coefficient,
in the fixed chart \(r=uz\), is

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_1={}&R_1(r)+zN_1(r)+z^2E_2(r)+z^3E_3,\\
R_1={}&\frac7{36}r^3-\frac7{16}r^4+\frac{13}{40}r^5
       -\frac7{96}r^6-\frac3{448}r^7,\\
N_1={}&\frac7{24}r^2-\frac7{16}r^3+\frac5{32}r^4
       +\frac1{64}r^5,\\
E_2={}&\frac1{12}r-\frac1{16}r^2-\frac1{48}r^3,\\
E_3={}&\frac1{36}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(zN_1(r)\) is the unique least odd-normal layer of the selected
radial-only decomposition.  In particular,
its top radial coefficient is \(r^5z/64\).  This is the correct normalized
background term to use when auditing a radial-prefix attack.  It is not a
class after the complete tangent graph is imposed.  The late source-only
coefficient \(s^8z^7\) is also nonzero, but it is not the first terminal and
is unnecessary for the prefix theorem.

The radial identity excludes cancellation internal to an arbitrary finite
radial Newton face.  The complete coupled calculation instead kills its
minimal defect-one descendant.  The first nonzero normalized coupled datum
is a normal-two/normal-three covariant defect displayed below.  Proving a
rate-two tail now requires an all-row recurrence for that defect; it cannot
be inferred from the radial word.

## Exact normalized background

Let \(L_s\) be the exact source-only Hamiltonian and use the source-control
convention

\[
H_s=L_s+8K_s(P_s,Q_s).
\]

Direct substitution in the exact family gives

\[
L_0+8\left(-\frac1{36}P_0^3-\frac14Q_0^2\right)=0.
\]

Expanding the same rational identity to coefficient \(s^1\) gives the four
layers displayed above.  Their odd-normal support is exactly \(\{1,3\}\),
and the row-zero coefficient has vanished.  Consequently a contribution
from the normal-three layer would need one more radial adjoint occurrence to
reach the normal order occupied by an \(n=1\) descendant.  At the minimal
parameter vertex this costs a positive parameter order and cannot collide
with the radial-only row-one normal-one descendant.  This observation is
valid before adjoining the prefix's tangent companion.  The next section
shows why the complete descendant nevertheless vanishes.

## Complete tangent-companion kill

For source Hamiltonian layers in the \((r,z)\) chart one has the exact
bracket identity

\[
[z^mh(r),z^nk(r)]
=z^{m+n-2}\bigl(mh k'-nh'k\bigr).
\]

Put \(T(r)=2-3r\).  The normalized row-one background obeys

\[
\boxed{R_1'=T N_1.}
\]

Every row-zero target pullback, written

\[
K(P_0,Q_0)=R(r)+zN(r)+O(z^2),
\]

obeys the same tangent graph

\[
\boxed{R'=T N.}
\]

The complete normal-minus-one bracket coefficient is therefore

\[
\boxed{
[H_1,K(P_0,Q_0)]_{n=-1}
=N_1R'-R_1'N=0.}
\]

This pairs the radial-word path with the path in which the prefix supplies
its normal-one companion and the background supplies its radial layer.  The
two have identical parameter order, normal order, and radial Newton
coordinate.  Their cancellation is polynomial and includes all tied radial
terms.  Once this first defect-one bracket is zero, further radial adjoints
do not restore the proposed minimal ray.

The next normalized coupled coefficient is not zero.  With the displayed
\(E_2,E_3\), define

\[
\Delta(H_1)=T^2E_3-T E_2'+2E_2.
\]

Exact simplification gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(H_1)
=-\frac{33r^3+18r^2-48r+8}{144}\ne0.}
\]

This is the correct successor carrier.  Corrections with zero radial
restriction are multiples of the seed cusp polynomial and satisfy
\(\Delta=0\); a nonzero radial correction changes \(\Delta\) through the
nonzero radial-to-compatibility symbol.  More precisely, if a current
contact-zero column has radial leader \(\kappa r^w\), then

\[
\boxed{
[r^{w-4}]\Delta\bigl(K(P_0,Q_0)\bigr)
=\frac{w(w-2)(w-3)}9\,\kappa.}
\]

This multiplier is nonzero for every cone weight \(w\ge5\).  Hence the
radial diagonal and the compatibility diagonal are coupled rather than
independent: cancelling a highest radial row installs a determined
normal-two/normal-three coefficient four radial degrees lower.  The missing
induction is an all-parameter-row recurrence for this covariant defect and
its higher-normal successors, including the lower-row Magnus terms at the
same coefficient.

## Conditional mixed highest-face identity

Let a finite radial prefix have primitive \(F(s,r)\), with
\(F(0,r)=0\), and put

\[
D(s,r)=\partial_rF(s,r)
      =\sum_{j\ge1,d\ge0}a_{j,d}s^jr^d.
\]

Give a monomial of \(D\) slope-two surplus

\[
\epsilon(j,d)=d-j-1,
\qquad
\epsilon=\max_{a_{j,d}\ne0}\epsilon(j,d).
\]

Assume \(\epsilon\ge0\).  Let \(j_0\) be the least parameter exponent on
the \(\epsilon\)-face.  The face equation forces a unique radial exponent

\[
d_0=j_0+1+\epsilon,
\]

and let \(a_0=a_{j_0,d_0}\ne0\).  For any odd \(n\) and nonzero
\(g(r)\), radial commutativity gives

\[
e^{-\operatorname{ad}_F}(z^ng)
=\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}
 \prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n-2i)\,
 z^{n-2k}D^kg.
\]

If \(\gamma r^a\) is the highest radial monomial of the first nonzero
parameter coefficient of \(g\), then the coefficient at the minimal face
vertex after \(k\) occurrences is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\gamma\,\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}
\left(\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(n-2i)\right)a_0^k
}
\]

at

\[
s^{\beta+kj_0}r^{a+kd_0}z^{n-2k}.
\]

It is nonzero for every \(k\): the falling factors never vanish for odd
\(n\), \(a_0\ne0\), and the coefficient ring has characteristic zero.
This resolves mixed tied-face cancellation inside the radial-only actor.
Terms of the same face with
larger parameter exponent cannot enter the least occurrence order; terms
below the face cannot reach its radial exponent.  Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{in}_\epsilon(D^k)
=\operatorname{in}_\epsilon(D)^k\ne0
\]

in the domain \(\mathbb Q[s,r]\), and its least-\(s\) vertex is the
\(k\)-th power of the least-\(s\) vertex of
\(\operatorname{in}_\epsilon(D)\).

For the normalized terminal one takes

\[
(\beta,n,a,\gamma)=\left(1,1,5,\frac1{64}\right).
\]

The velocity occurrence orders and source Hamiltonian degrees are

\[
N_k=1+kj_0,
\qquad
\deg H_{N_k}=11+2k(d_0-1).
\]

After the density-\(z^2\) Hamiltonian-to-vector translation, the transported
velocity coordinates have limiting rate

\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}
\frac{\deg X_{H_{N_k}}}{N_k+1}
=\frac{2(d_0-1)}{j_0}
=2+\frac{2\epsilon}{j_0}\ge2.
\]

The occurrence map \(k\mapsto 1+kj_0\) is injective.  This becomes an
asymptotic payment only after two additional premises are proved: the
complete tangent/current quotient retains the coordinate, and the typed
velocity-to-logarithm response is nonzero on an infinite subsequence.  The
first premise fails on the defect-one face above.  Existing exact replay of
a finite supercritical prefix also shows that an infinite forward-`dexp`
velocity response can correspond to a logarithm with no higher terminal
rows, so the second premise cannot be replaced by a bounded order shift.

## Exact current-row counterattack

The normal-one coordinate is not a class of the complete contact-zero
current quotient.  At row one, put

\[
K_1=
\frac{29}{420}Q^2
-\frac{19}{840}PQ
-\frac1{168}P^2Q
-\frac{47}{2520}P^3.
\]

Every monomial lies in the target-lift-compatible contact-zero algebra.
Exact substitution at \(s=0\) gives

\[
H_1+8K_1(P_0,Q_0)
=-\frac{z^3}{5040}
\left(
45u^3z^2-207u^2z+15uz+258u-46
\right).
\]

In \((r,z)\) coordinates this is

\[
z^2\left(
-\frac{43}{840}r+\frac{23}{560}r^2-\frac1{112}r^3
\right)
+z^3\left(\frac{23}{2520}-\frac1{336}r\right).
\]

Hence this current row cancels both the radial and normal-one blocks, but
leaves the nonzero odd-normal terminal

\[
\boxed{z^3\frac{46-15r}{5040}}.
\]

The counterattack therefore does not cancel the radial Newton cascade by a
tied radial word.  It changes the displayed odd layer while the tangent
graph has already killed the proposed defect-one cascade.  Its surviving
normal-two/normal-three covariant coefficient is

\[
\Delta
=-\frac{9r^3-36r^2+39r-10}{72}\ne0.
\]

A complete proof must follow this covariant coefficient, rather than attach
an automatic payment to the new odd-normal term.

## Inverse-factor and local-smoothing audit: scope correction

The covariant coefficient \(\Delta\) is not a one-dimensional
\(T^1\)-smoothing class on which every finite contact-zero prefix acts by a
unit.  The apparent identification confuses a jet on the normalization with
a deformation class of the plane cusp.

At \(s=0\), the radial seed curve has equation

\[
C(P,Q)=4P^3-P^2-18PQ+27Q^2+4Q=0
\]

and a cusp at

\[
(P_*,Q_*)=\left(\frac13,\frac1{27}\right),
\qquad r_*=\frac23.
\]

Writing \(x=P-P_*\), \(y=Q-Q_*\), its local equation is

\[
C=4x^3+3x^2-18xy+27y^2.
\]

The Jacobian quotient is

\[
T^1_C
=\frac{\mathbb Q[x,y]}{(C_x,C_y)}
\cong\frac{\mathbb Q[x]}{(x^2)},
\]

because \(C_y=-18(x-3y)\) and the remaining relation is \(x^2=0\).
It is two-dimensional, not canonically one-dimensional.

Put \(\tau=3r-2\).  The exact normalization is

\[
x=-\frac{\tau^2}{12},
\qquad
y=-\frac{\tau^2(\tau+3)}{108}.
\]

Therefore the map from polynomial \(T^1\) representatives to the
normalization first-jet algebra

\[
J_{\rm ram}=\mathbb Q[\tau]/(\tau^2)
\]

sends the nonconstant class \(x\) to zero.  Only the constant smoothing
line reaches \(J_{\rm ram}\).  By contrast, the normalized covariant
coefficient has jet

\[
[\Delta(H_1)]_{J_{\rm ram}}
=\frac7{162}-\frac5{108}\tau.
\]

Its nonzero \(\tau\)-coefficient is a ramification jet, not a polynomial
\(T^1\)-smoothing direction.

There is also an explicit annihilator of the unnormalized jet.  Exact
substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(P_0^3)
&=-\frac{27r^2-36r+8}{8}
 \equiv\frac12\pmod{\tau^2},\\
\Delta(P_0Q_0)
&=\frac{5r-2}{8}
 \equiv\frac16+\frac5{24}\tau\pmod{\tau^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the admissible finite contact-zero correction

\[
K_*=-\frac{13}{648}P^3+\frac1{36}PQ,
\]

one obtains the polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
\Delta\bigl(H_1+8K_*(P_0,Q_0)\bigr)
=-\frac{(3r-2)^2(33r-16)}{1296}.}
\]

Consequently its class in \(J_{\rm ram}\) is zero before radial
renormalization.  The two columns \(P^3\) and \(PQ\) span the value and
first-jet directions of that isolated map.  This calculation by itself says
nothing about the fiber in which the radial equation has already been fixed.

This annihilator is not a radial-normalized current row.  Its exact radial
restriction is

\[
K_*(P_0,Q_0)|_{z=0}
=\frac{r^3(3r-4)(117r^2-240r+136)}{41472}\ne0.
\]

It also restores a nonzero first-normal block.  Hence it proves only that
the isolated ramification jet is not invariant under the complete
unnormalized contact-zero action.  It does not erase the coupled
radial/normal obstruction while preserving the already-imposed radial
equations.  If two current corrections have the same radial restriction,
their difference is a multiple of \(C\); the exact kernel calculation then
gives \(\Delta(C G)=0\).  Consequently, after the radial row has been fixed,
the value of \(\Delta\) is independent of the chosen polynomial lift.

The corrected conclusion is therefore two-sided: \(\Delta\) is not an
absolute local jet invariant, but it is the canonical cokernel coordinate
of the coupled radial-normal current solve.  A later-row prefix can still
act on this coordinate through Magnus brackets, which is the remaining
all-order problem.

## Radially normalized critical-kernel response

The slope-two critical algebra resolves the temporal scope left open by the
preceding audit.  Put

\[
x=sr,\qquad \eta=\frac{z}{r^2},\qquad
D=4P^3+27Q^2.
\]

Here \(D\), rather than the full fixed-chart polynomial \(C\), generates the
same-row radial/\(\Delta\) kernel.  For the exact critical pullback, write

\[
D(P_{\rm crit},Q_{\rm crit})
=d_0(x)+\eta d_1(x)+\eta^2d_2(x)+\eta^3d_3(x)+O(\eta^4).
\]

The coefficients needed for the first nonzero response are

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_0&=\frac{27}{128}x-\frac{333}{4096}x^2+\frac1{128}x^3,\\
d_1&=-\frac{63}{128}x+\frac{111}{512}x^2
      -\frac{111}{2048}x^3+O(x^4),\\
d_2&=-\frac9{16}+\frac38x-\frac{105}{512}x^2+O(x^3),\\
d_3&=\frac12-\frac3{32}x+\frac3{32}x^2+O(x^3).
\end{aligned}
\]

Let a finite critical-kernel column be

\[
K=D^qG,
\qquad q\ge1,
\]

where \(G\) is weighted homogeneous of weight \(m\), is not divisible by
\(D\), and

\[
g_0=G\!\left(-\frac34,-\frac14\right)\ne0.
\]

The last inequality follows from maximality of \(q\).  Indeed the weighted
parametrization

\[
P=-\frac34T^2,\qquad Q=-\frac14T^3
\]

has kernel \((D)\).  A weight-\(m\) polynomial maps to
\(T^mG(-3/4,-1/4)\), so vanishing at the displayed seed point is equivalent
to divisibility by \(D\).  The total weight of \(K\) is \(m+6q\), so it
occurs at target row

\[
J=m+6q-6.
\]

Set \(A=27/128\).  Direct coefficient extraction in the exact critical
polynomials gives the following triangular response.

1. There is no radial response before delay \(q\).  The possible raw
   \(\Delta\)-response at delay \(q-1\) cancels identically.
2. At delay \(q\), the radial coefficient is \(g_0A^q\).  If \(H\) is any
   weight-\(m+7q\) radial normalizer with seed value \(h_0\ne0\), its
   coefficient is fixed by

   \[
   \frac{\kappa_1h_0}{g_0}=-A^q.
   \]

   After this normalization, the complete \(\Delta\)-coefficient at the
   same delay is again zero.
3. Normalize the next radial row with any nonzero-diagonal homogeneous
   column.  The resulting target-level coefficient at delay \(q+1\) is

   \[
   \boxed{
   \Delta_{J+q+1}^{\rm tgt}(D^qG)
   =g_0\,qA^{q-1}\frac{9(3m+21q-17)}{256}.}
   \]

   In the source Hamiltonian convention \(H=L+8K(P,Q)\), this becomes

   \[
   \boxed{
   \Delta_{J+q+1}^{\rm src}(D^qG)
   =g_0\,qA^{q-1}\frac{9(3m+21q-17)}{32}.}
   \]

The multiplier never vanishes in characteristic zero: \(m\ge0\),
\(q\ge1\), and \(3m+21q-17\ge4\).

For completeness, the cancellation is independent of the selected radial
sections.  It suffices by linearity to take

\[
G=P^aQ^b,\qquad H=P^cQ^d,
\]

with \(2a+3b=m\) and \(2c+3d=m+7q\).  After the first normalizer, the next
radial coefficient divided by \(g_0\) is

\[
A^q\frac{-4a+4c+5q}{96}.
\]

The second normalizer cancels this value.  Substitution into
\((3(J+q+1)+8)A_{J+q+1}+9B_{J+q+1}\) eliminates \(a\), \(c\), and the
choice of the second section, leaving exactly the boxed formula.  The
monomial calculation extends to every homogeneous \(G,H\) by linearity.

For \(q=1\), the first normalized delay cancels and the second response is

\[
\Delta_{m+2}^{\rm tgt}(DG)=g_0\frac{9(3m+4)}{256},
\]

which is the smallest member of the all-\(q\) identity.

This proves that a single nonzero finite critical-kernel column cannot be
absorbed by recursive radial normalization: its maximal \(D\)-adic factor
only postpones the nonzero velocity-quotient response to delay \(q+1\).
For an arbitrary finite prefix, several columns can have the same activation
row \(J+q+1\), so their boxed coefficients can still cancel.  The remaining
temporal problem is the injectivity of the finite activation convolution,
followed by the typed velocity-to-Magnus passage.  Neither step is replaced
by this single-column theorem.

This also separates \(\Delta\) from the inverse-factor/Rees node--cusp
obstruction.  The completed reciprocal factor \(1-zW\) is itself a unit and
can be flattened by a change of monogenic presentation.  The surviving
Rees theorem is global: it compares the two normalization points of a node
with the unibranch seed cusp and uses their branch-value separation.  It is
not the local \(T^1\) class at \(r=2/3\), and it supplies no unit action on
the sequence of \(\Delta\)-jets.  The finite-supercritical-prefix arm of the
Rees dichotomy remains exactly the loophole exhibited here.

## Kill conditions and boundary

The radial-only identity would fail if the primitive had a parameter-order
zero term, if the maximal face had \(\epsilon<0\), or if the coefficient
ring had positive characteristic causing an odd falling factor to vanish.
None occurs in the normalized characteristic-zero contact category.  Its
use as a payment transition is nevertheless killed by the complete tangent
companion and by the velocity/logarithm distinction.

In particular, this artifact does not promote the source-only
\(s^8z^7\) coordinate, a finite replay, or an isolated Lie word to the
unrestricted lower bound.  It supplies an all-index conditional radial-face
identity, rejects the defect-one finite-prefix payment edge, and identifies
the nonzero covariant coefficient that the root radial/normal induction must
follow.
