## Attack vectors and counterattacks

1. **Initial-grade separation.**  Compute \(h_m\) exactly and solve the two
   zero-grade equations over \(m,j\).  A single admissible solution kills the
   easy separation theorem.
2. **Coupled transport.**  Even when every initial backbone letter is lower,
   brackets among lower letters and current positive-contact controls might
   reach the terminal grade.  Additivity must be checked on the complete
   quotient, including moving-relation velocities.  A held-out same-grade
   bracket kills coefficientwise separation.
3. **Cancellation with paid surplus.**  A positive-grade stabilizer may
   cancel the chosen terminal while creating a different equal-or-higher
   shell.  The correct verdict is then a dichotomy, not invariance of the
   chosen representative.  The compiler must return the surplus witness.
4. **Higher parameter rows.**  Independent \(H_m\) coefficients at every
   order might form an infinite triangular cancellation schedule.  Any proof
   must be uniform in \(m,j\); stabilization in a finite window is
   insufficient.
