# Prior-art boundary for the equivariant rigidity calculation

**Question.** Has the public degree-`(7,6,4)` Jacobian map already been
shown to be locally rigid, modulo scaling, in either its complete same-degree
equivariant coefficient chart or the complete first omitted degree shell, or
has its filtered Hamiltonian tangency already been computed?

## Closest sources checked

1. Alexis Gallagher, [Weighted lifts from the Jacobian
   counterexample](https://github.com/algal/jacobianfun/blob/main/RESEARCH.md),
   dated 2026-07-20.  This derives the invariant coordinates, the quotient
   Jacobian mechanism, a one-variable weighted-lift family, all generic fiber
   degrees at least three, and exact collision atlases.  It contains no
   coefficient deformation, tangent-space, obstruction, or rigidity result.
   It does explicitly derive the inverse polynomial, the rule that a seed of
   degree `d` has generic fiber degree `d+1`, and the escape of repeated roots
   through `gamma=0`. Those parts of our calculation are recoveries.

2. T. Shaska, [Graded Keller maps and the Jacobian
   Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20210), submitted 2026-07-22.  Its
   Theorem 6.1 supplies the ambient identity used here: for the
   `(1,-1,-2)` grading, the Keller condition descends to a quotient Jacobian
   vanishing to order two on the contracted locus.  The paper proposes a
   classification by weight signature and describes the three-dimensional
   hyperbolic middle as open.  Searches of the paper for `rigid`, `deform`,
   and `infinitesimal` returned no match, and no same-degree local
   classification is stated.

3. [A Counterexample to the Jacobian
   Conjecture](https://www.ulam.ai/research/jacobian.pdf), dated 2026-07-20.
   Its Section 5 gives two broad classes of Keller-map families: triangular
   source-coordinate deformations and polynomial families realizing every
   generic degree at least three. These are essential prior art, but the paper
   does not form the normalized equivariant coefficient scheme, compute its
   tangent or obstruction spaces, or state local rigidity in a bounded degree
   shell. The existence of those families also fixes the claim boundary: a
   bounded equivariant rigidity result cannot be promoted to rigidity among
   arbitrary Keller maps.

4. P. Migus, [Generic degrees of real polynomial Keller maps with non-dense
   image](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21572), dated 2026-07-23, explicitly uses
   members of the public deformation family. It studies attainable generic
   degrees and non-dense real images, rather than the local coefficient scheme
   at the public map.

5. Targeted searches for equivariant/graded Keller-map deformation and local
   rigidity located general work on Keller maps and polynomial automorphisms,
   but no statement tied to this newly announced map or its exact
   degree-`(7,6,4)` coefficient scheme or adjacent equivariant shell.

6. A second targeted arXiv search for Keller-map Hamiltonian deformations,
   infinitesimal equivariant deformations, and the weighted-lift family found
   no relevant source beyond the three July 2026 items above. The returned
   Hamiltonian-deformation papers concerned unrelated symplectic or
   Lagrangian geometry. This narrows the search record; it does not certify
   historical priority.

## Disposition

The generic-degree and branch-at-infinity mechanism is **public recovery**.
The two bounded local-rigidity statements and the five-shell Hamiltonian
tangency are **absent from the closest public record found**, but literature
priority is not certified. The same is true of the newly derived second-order
escape from polynomial target Hamiltonians plus fixed-gamma source shears.
The strongest current wording is:

> Exact rational deformation calculations make the public map formally
> isolated, modulo two scaling motions, through the full same-degree
> equivariant chart and the complete first omitted degree shell. The adjacent
> shell briefly opens an extra tangent and a second-order escape, both killed
> by exact higher obstruction classes. Across the complete cumulative support
> filtration, the known cubic family first becomes tangent at shell five, and
> its tangent is the Hamiltonian field generated by
> `-Q^2/4 - P^3/36`. These bounded statements were not found in the closest
> public analyses and coexist with known deformation families outside the
> chart.

The second-jet calculation further shows that this first-order Hamiltonian
contact cannot be continued to order two by any polynomial target
Hamiltonian and any polynomial volume-preserving source shear fixing
`gamma`. Broader equivariant source-coordinate actions remain outside that
statement.

Do not call it the first proof until a specialist checks the coefficient
chart, the chosen equivalence group, and older deformation literature. Do not
present the generic-degree certificate as a discovery.

## Rees node/cusp and contact-complexity follow-up

The later all-order lane was checked separately against the same closest
public record.

- Gallagher's weighted-lift note contains no occurrence of `node`, `cusp`,
  `deform`, or `Rees`.  It owns the weighted-lift construction, generic
  degrees, inverse equations, and collision data.
- The public counterexample manuscript contains no occurrence of `node`,
  `cusp`, `deformation`, or `Hamiltonian`.
- The current full text of Shaska's grading paper contains no occurrence of
  `node`, `cusp`, `deform`, or `Hamiltonian`.  Its discriminant and quotient
  results remain essential ambient prior art.
- Migus's note uses the public deformation family to classify attainable
  real generic degrees; it contains no node, cusp, or Rees analysis.
- Fong and Fable's state note reports capped local deformation calculations
  and torus-rigidity evidence.  It does not state the diagonal Rees
  degeneration, the node-versus-cusp contact obstruction, or a
  source/target logarithmic degree minimax.
