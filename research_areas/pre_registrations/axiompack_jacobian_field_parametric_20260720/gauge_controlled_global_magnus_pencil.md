# Global Magnus audit of the two-control connection

**Status:** exact finite replay completed; associated-graded recurrence open

## Eigenquestion

For the regular target control

\[
K_s=a(s)P^3+b(s)PQ,
\]

the target Hamiltonians lie in the two-dimensional Poisson algebra
\(\langle P^3,PQ\rangle\), and the target-relative source connection
\(W_s\) restricts to translations on the exceptional divisor.  Does the
full two-variable Magnus logarithm

\[
\Omega_s=\log\operatorname{Pexp}\!\int_0^sW_u\,du
\]

have asymptotic ordinary excess slope below two?

A positive answer would give an all-order noncanonical contact beating the
canonical root chart.  A negative answer must identify an off-divisor
associated-graded shell that survives the complete two-control image.

## Attack

1. Expand the exact rational connection \(W_s\) coefficientwise.
2. Compute the multivariate Magnus recursion with the same left/right
   conventions used in the divisor replay.
3. Record the total degree and leading homogeneous pair at each order.
4. Compute the Lie algebra generated by the first coefficient fields at the
   associated-graded level.  Test whether the observed top shell comes from
   repeated adjoint action of the finite prefix \(W_0\).
5. Compare with the fixed-first-slice connection obtained by adding
   \(-Q^2/4\).  If the shell disappears only after changing the finite
   prefix, it is a `dexp` coordinate effect rather than an invariant.

## Counterattacks

- Divisor translation does not control transverse brackets.
- The closed target algebra \(\langle P^3,PQ\rangle\) does not imply that
  its polynomial source pullbacks form a fixed finite-dimensional Lie
  algebra as \(s\) varies.
- A finite high-degree \(W_0\) can generate a linear Magnus slope even when
  every velocity coefficient has uniformly bounded degree.
- A finite prefix computation can falsify a proposed slope bound, but cannot
  prove an all-order upper bound without a bracket-closure or recurrence
  theorem.

## Discriminators

- If the generated source fields close in a finite-dimensional polynomial
  Lie algebra, the source logarithm has uniformly bounded degree and the
  symmetric slope is zero for this contact.
- If a unique top homogeneous chain has excess increment \(c\) per adjoint,
  its nonvanishing gives a candidate source slope \(c\), subject to
  cancellation by the remaining target image.
- If the computed top shell changes between left and right conventions or
  between equivalent finite-prefix gauges, it cannot be promoted as a
  contact invariant.

## Claim boundary

The exact source-only connection already proves that the admissible polar
image spans the Rees node motion.  This calculation concerns the logarithm
of one explicit global target-relative connection.  It can disprove
symmetric slope two by construction, but a slope-two result for this one
gauge would not prove minimax optimality.

## Exact finite replay

Write

\[
g=2t-3v.
\]

For the two-control connection without the fixed first slice, the source
velocity degrees through order six are

\[
9,11,13,15,15,15,15,
\]

while the previously computed opposite-orientation logarithm degrees
through order seven are

\[
9,11,11,18,20,34,36.
\]

That preliminary prefix used the target-side Magnus sign for the source
factor and is superseded as a source-flow calculation.  It remains only a
degree diagnostic.

Adding the fixed target slice \(-Q^2/4\) makes the source velocity at
parameter zero vanish.  For the source equation

\[
\partial_s\psi_s=D\psi_s\,V_s,
\]

the velocity multiplies the flow on the right, so
\(\operatorname{dexp}^{-1}\) has first bracket coefficient \(+1/2\).
Its source velocity degrees at parameter orders
\(0,\ldots,7\) are

\[
-\infty,11,13,15,15,15,15,15,
\]

and its source-flow Magnus logarithm degrees at orders \(1,\ldots,8\)
are

\[
-\infty,11,13,15,17,22,24,26.
\]

The leading homogeneous source fields before the first orientation-sensitive
bracket, at orders two through four, are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_2^{\rm top}
  &=\frac{3}{128}v^6g^4(-v,t-3v),\\
\Omega_3^{\rm top}
  &=-\frac{7}{768}v^7g^5(-v,t-3v),\\
\Omega_4^{\rm top}
  &=\frac{1}{1024}v^8g^6(-v,t-3v).
\end{aligned}
\]

At order six a new transverse ray appears:

The next two exact top fields are

\[

In the \((v,g)\) chart, these are the weighted-Hamiltonian fields for

\[
\frac{1}{1048576}v^{13}g^{12},\qquad
-\frac{619}{1321205760}v^{14}g^{13},\qquad

\[
X_H=

\]

Thus all three are nonzero scalar multiples of the ray

\[
v^{n+7}g^{n+6}.
\]

Consequently their degrees are exactly \(2n+10\).  This supplies a concrete
associated-graded recurrence candidate, but three nonzero coefficients do
not prove its persistence.

The target Hamiltonian

\[
K_s=a(s)P^3+b(s)PQ-\frac14Q^2
\]

has left Magnus ordinary-degree profile through order thirteen

\[
3,3,3,3,4,4,5,5,6,6,7,7,8.
\]

Its weighted \((4,6)\)-degree profile is

\[
12,12,14,14,18,18,22,22,26,26,30,30,34.
\]

For this explicit contact the source side therefore dominates the observed
finite prefix.  The next discriminator is an exact recurrence or a
cancellation of the ray \(v^{n+7}g^{n+6}\); extrapolation from the degree
list is insufficient.

## Precise finite claim and kill condition

The finite claim is only this: over \(\mathbb Q\), for the displayed
fixed-slice target control and the source-flow convention, the homogeneous
projections of \(\Omega_6,\Omega_7,\Omega_8\) are exactly the three nonzero
fields displayed above.  No asymptotic degree statement follows from these
three coefficients.

The proposed all-order recurrence

\[
\Omega_n^{\rm top}=X_{c_n v^{n+7}g^{n+6}},
\qquad c_n\ne0,\qquad n\ge6,
\]

is killed by any exact order \(n\ge9\) for which:

1. the degree of \(\Omega_n\) is not \(2n+10\);
2. its degree-\((2n+10)\) projection is not contained in the displayed
   one-dimensional ray; or
3. the ray coefficient \(c_n\) vanishes.

Even an all-order proof for this connection would describe one gauge.  It
would not give a minimax lower bound over all admissible contacts and hence
would add no conclusion beyond the established
\(\sigma_{\rm ct}\le2\) upper bound.  The ray is therefore a diagnostic for
the remaining gauge search, not a new asymptotic theorem.
