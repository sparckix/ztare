Chat is not the ledger.

The conversation is a user interface. Authority lives in roles, mandates, gates, claims, telemetry, and experiment rows.

Three objects. Different authority.

ZTARE separates persistent offices from disposable model calls and from the human principal. That distinction is the governance kernel.

01
Principal

Owns taste, budgets, public claims, irreversible decisions, and risk acceptance. The system reduces babysitting; it does not pretend legal accountability moved to a model.

02
Role office

A persistent agent identity such as Manager or Research Director. It can claim work, refuse scope, open gates, send handoffs, and close experiments within a mandate.

03
Transient invocation

A cold-shot, mutator, judge, or one-off LLM call. It produces evidence or proposals. It has no standing authority unless promoted into a role office.

1 Preferences Principal declares what is worth attention: hard problems, money/prize potential, architecture fit, governance learning.
2 Role mandate Each office gets allowed paths, forbidden actions, budget caps, escalation duties, and closure rules.
3 Work discovery Tasks, damage signals, inbox messages, and experiment debt become candidates; critical damage outranks routine work.
4 Decision card Orbit or Telegram asks for natural-language approval only when the action needs human authority.
5 Closure Artifacts, telemetry, result markers, E/F/INS rows, and transition logs make the outcome replayable.

Human-agent communication should feel like a board packet, not a terminal.

The natural interface is text. The system still records structured state underneath so approvals are auditable and agents cannot quietly mutate scope.

One backend, many projections.

Orbit, Telegram, and CLI are projections over the same state: ztare_workspace/gates/, org/channels/, org/tasks/, and the transition log. None of them owns a separate truth.

Orbit

Rich governance UI for pending gates, role inboxes, context, trade-offs, and artifact links. Best for considered approvals.

Telegram

Compact push channel for urgent approvals and completion/failure notices. Best for “approve/skip/ask/stop” while away from the repo.

A2A inbox

Durable role-to-role envelopes. Messages create obligations, not execution authority; work still needs authorization, claim, and closure.

CLI

Bootstrap, debugging, and direct operator-console surface. Use it when the principal wants to talk to Claude/Codex directly without starting the daemon.

Working today. Hardening tomorrow.

The single-team research loop runs end to end. Enterprise-grade deployment needs a stronger control plane, auth, leases, and production UI hardening.

Works today

First-run setup, role preflight, daemon dry-run, Research Director mandate, principal preferences, task claims, executive gates, Orbit/Telegram projections, A2A-style role cards, damage signals, and external-run watchdogs.

Still not enterprise-complete

Multi-tenant backend, production auth, SSO/RBAC, signed audit, idempotent gate resolution, constrained execution containers, retention policy, and packaged agent runtimes remain open.
